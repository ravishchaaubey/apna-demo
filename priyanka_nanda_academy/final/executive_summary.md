## Where PNA is today

PNA is a working bridal studio in Sector 8, Chandigarh, with founder-taught, on-demand makeup classes on the side. It teaches fewer than 60 paying students a year [FOUNDER] at a fee we do not know (modelled at ₹60,000 incl. kit against a local band of ₹35–75k) [HYP]; on the reconciled tricity market that is about 7% of makeup-led learners, illustrative (3–12%) [EST]. Our research tools found a website and nothing else: no Google rating, no Instagram, no reviews, no published fees or outcomes, and no presence in any generic search we could run. **That finding is not verifiable from our environment** (Instagram, Google Maps and Justdial were blocked and the site refused our connection), so the founder confirms her real footprint on day 0 [FACT of absence, tool-limited]. The founder does all the teaching, counselling and selling, and wants to step back from daily teaching within two to three years [FOUNDER]. She is willing to invest ₹10–50 lakh over the next 12 months [FOUNDER].

## The recommendation [REC]

1. **Build one Chandigarh Flagship:** a trainer-delivered, small-batch (≤12), bridal-specialist academy with a published course ladder (₹999 Career Taster → ₹39,900 Foundation → Professional Bridal Makeup Artist Programme → masterclasses → Pro Circle), a counsellor-run WhatsApp funnel that replies within five minutes and closes at a parent visit, EMI, and live practice on real brides in the founder's studio. Positioning: *the small-batch bridal makeup academy inside a working bridal studio*, with fees (and the family's all-in cost), practice hours and alumni outcomes published so families can check before they pay.
2. **Spend the first rupee on proof and response discipline, not ads:** Google Business Profile, academy Instagram, CRM, portfolio-day shoots, an Alumni Outcomes Report by 31 January 2027.
3. **Raise the flagship price only after proof, in two steps:** ₹75,000 list the month after gate G1 (August 2027 in the planning case), then ₹90,000 list in Year 2 only if the first step did not cost more than 15% of conversion (₹80,000 realised is the planning fee). Price is the biggest lever and the least tested: a ₹19.7L swing on Year-1 profit, with no willingness-to-pay evidence yet and no price response in the model [EST]. Families compare all-in cost: about ₹0.67L today (fee incl. kit), rising to ₹0.97L at the full price without the optional kit, ₹1.17L with it [EST].
4. **Add the regional engine in Year 2, behind a gate:** a compressed "Destination" course (4 weeks online, 5–6 weeks in Chandigarh) for out-of-town students, vetted women-only accommodation, parents' orientation and a pop-up circuit in Ludhiana, Patiala, Jalandhar, Amritsar and Shimla. A six-student pilot runs in the August–September 2027 intakes; scale is released only when out-of-town demand is already ≥15% of enrolments and two pilot pop-ups convert.
5. **Do not do:** a beauty-services marketplace (loses ₹932 per booking; Urban Company took ~10 years to profit), an owned Delhi NCR or Ludhiana centre (−₹7.4L a year at 46 students), franchising now, a national online course now, joining a chain as a franchisee (erases the founder brand), or the ₹50L plan (−₹3.0L EBITDA on a ₹44.2L cash need). Partner-hosted satellites and licensing wait for later gates.
6. **Someone other than the founder runs it, and her load is disclosed.** An Admissions & Operations Lead is hired in November 2026 (counsellor pay plus a ₹15k/month premium) to own the 90-day plan and the weekly funnel review; the founder chairs the Friday plan review and the gate decisions. Her share of teaching falls from about 100% to ≤50% by April and ≤40% by October 2027, but her **teaching hours peak at about 114 a month in August 2027 in the planning case (about 128 in May if G1 comes early) and stay above 106 through October** [EST], before masterclasses, assessment and the studio. A second trainer joins in August; if her hours exceed 110 a month for two months, that hire is pulled forward. Written cover exists for her absence and for the Lead Trainer leaving. The slower step-back costs no cash in Year 1 but about 26 of her hours a month.

**Year 3 is a gated number.** ₹54.1L of the ₹162.9L (33%) depends on price step 2, the regional engine and an online audience PNA does not yet have. Without them the flagship earns ₹108.8L and roughly zero EBITDA (−₹10.9L after the founder's salary). **The founder's cash comes back in month 29 before her own salary (IRR 46% to month 36; 87% to month 60 with no growth after Year 3 and no terminal value) and in month 40 after it (IRR −10% to month 36; 33% to month 60).** If G1 were passed in April instead of July (the upside, which the plan's own numbers do not support), Year 1 would be ₹66.7L and −₹0.8L with 91 enrolments. The boutique alternative (₹10L plan) earns +₹10.8L a year on ₹39.8L with ₹3.1L of peak cash and the founder teaching everything.

Unit economics at the full price: an L3 seat contributes ₹50.4k before acquisition cost and a 12-seat batch breaks even at 3.6 students; at the step-1 price ₹39.6k and 38% of a batch; at today's assumed fee ₹22.2k and 8 students [EST]. The business breaks even at about 7.8 career enrolments a month on the month-12 cost base, which carries the second trainer and the Operations Lead [EST]. The founder's unpaid teaching is today's margin: replacing it costs about ₹10L in Year 1 and ₹17–19L a year thereafter, plus about ₹8L a year for an academy manager from Year 2 [EST]. GST treatment is worth about ₹10.9L a year on the planning case and needs a chartered accountant's opinion.

## Investment: plan to need ₹25 lakh, authorise ₹35 lakh, be ready to lose up to ₹33 lakh [REC]

- **Tranche 1, authorise now: up to ₹15L** (expected use ₹11.8L by June 2027). Admissions & Operations Lead (November), Lead Trainer (December–January), assistant, content and video, CRM/WhatsApp, website, legal/accounting/B&WSSC, advertising capped at ₹15k/month until G0 and ₹60k/month until G1, basic recording of theory (₹1L), a weekend/evening batch slot in the studio from March. No new premises. **Hard rule:** monthly cash review from February; at the March review, if cash is on the adverse track (₹15L already spent), advertising cost per enrolment is above ₹12k, or the 1 March batch starts with fewer than 4 students, the team is cut to a stop-loss state.
- **Tranche 2, release only at G1 (decided in the last week of July 2027 on May–July actuals): up to ₹15L more.** Lease and fit-out booked in July (fit-out ₹10.4L, deposit ₹3.1L, stations ₹1.2L: ₹14.7L committed), move in August, second trainer, advertising to ₹1.0L/month from September, price step 1, Destination pilot, two regional pop-up pilots. **No lease is signed before that reading**, and the lease carries no more than three months' notice.
- **Contingency: ₹5L above the ₹30L.** It covers what the model's cash excludes (income tax, GST timing, working capital) and, if the founder draws her ₹75k/month from the academy rather than the studio, her salary. It is drawn only on her sign-off, never for new scope.
- **The ₹35–50L band is not a Year-1 budget.** It is reserved for the regional engine in Year 2 (₹4–10L) and released only at gate G2.

**What ₹35L can lose.** If the funnel fails and nobody acts, the Tranche-1 team burns about ₹2.3L a month at adverse rates and the loss reaches **about ₹36L by October 2027; it becomes visible in February 2027**, when cash crosses the ₹15L cap. With the mandatory March cut (KT-1a) it is **about ₹25L (₹25.4L)**, still burning about ₹0.9L a month, so a failed Tranche 1 means reverting to the boutique model rather than waiting. If G1 passes on a false positive and the peak season then fails after the move, the loss is **about ₹33L, visible in September 2027**, of which ₹11.6L of fit-out is sunk and the ₹3.1L deposit is recoverable; exiting the lease costs about ₹4.9L. Once the lease is signed, the floor is ₹32–33L; the only protection is reading G1 on actual May–July results, never on a forecast of them.

## The biggest risks

Price realisation (the biggest lever, untested, with a 45% rise in the family's all-in cost at the full price); the downside after the move (₹14.7L committed on the G1 reading; ≈₹33L if the peak season fails); the downside before it (≈₹36L if the funnel fails and the March cut is not made); the founder's load (about 114 hours of teaching at the August peak in the planning case, 128 in May on the early-G1 path, before everything else she does); nobody owning execution unless the Operations Lead is hired; optimistic funnel benchmarks (the cost range for the same enrolments is ~9x wide, none of it makeup-specific, the paid route is reconciled by a calibration factor, and the organic and masterclass volumes are unevidenced); hiring a senior bridal artist as a salaried trainer in wedding season at an unsourced ₹60k/month; capacity binding in the studio through May–July; GST and lease terms; an out-of-town safety incident; and misleading-claim exposure if "100% placement"-style promises creep in.
