## Where PNA is today

PNA is a working bridal studio in Sector 8, Chandigarh, with founder-taught, on-demand makeup classes on the side. It teaches fewer than 60 paying students a year [FOUNDER] at a fee we do not know (modelled at ₹60,000 incl. kit against a local band of ₹35–75k) [HYP]; on the reconciled tricity market that is about 7% of makeup-led learners, illustrative (3–12%) [EST]. It has a website and, as far as we could find, nothing else: no Google rating, no Instagram we could identify, no reviews, no published fees, no published outcomes, and it does not appear for any non-branded search we ran [FACT of absence]. The founder does all the teaching, counselling and selling, and wants to step back from daily teaching within two to three years [FOUNDER]. She is willing to invest ₹10–50 lakh over the next 12 months [FOUNDER].

## The recommendation [REC]

1. **Build one Chandigarh Flagship:** a trainer-delivered, small-batch (≤12), bridal-specialist academy with a published course ladder (₹999 Career Taster → ₹39,900 Foundation → Professional Bridal Makeup Artist Programme → masterclasses → Pro Circle), a counsellor-run WhatsApp funnel that replies within five minutes and closes at a parent visit, EMI, and live practice on real brides in the founder's studio. Positioning: *the small-batch bridal makeup academy inside a working bridal studio*, with fees, practice hours and alumni outcomes published so families can check before they pay.
2. **Spend the first rupee on proof and response discipline, not ads:** Google Business Profile, academy Instagram, CRM, portfolio-day shoots, an Alumni Outcomes Report by 31 January 2027.
3. **Raise the flagship price only after proof, in two steps:** ₹75,000 list the month after gate G1, then ₹90,000 list (₹80,000 realised is the planning fee). Price is the single biggest lever: a ₹23.0L swing on Year-1 profit [EST]. The two-step path itself costs about ₹4.9L of Year-1 profit against a single jump; that is the price of protecting conversion while proof is thin. Families compare all-in cost: about ₹0.67L today (fee incl. kit) rising to ₹0.97L at the full price without the optional kit, ₹1.17L with it [EST].
4. **Add the regional engine in Year 2, behind a gate:** a compressed "Destination" course (4 weeks online, 5–6 weeks in Chandigarh) for out-of-town students, vetted women-only accommodation, parents' orientation and a pop-up circuit in Ludhiana, Patiala, Jalandhar, Amritsar and Shimla. Released only when out-of-town demand is already ≥15% of enrolments and two pilot pop-ups convert.
5. **Do not do:** a beauty-services marketplace (loses ₹932 per booking; Urban Company took ~10 years to profit), an owned Delhi NCR or Ludhiana centre (−₹7.4L a year at 46 students), franchising now, a national online course now, or joining a chain as a franchisee (erases the founder brand). Partner-hosted satellites and licensing wait for later gates.
6. **The founder steps back by proof, not by calendar:** ≤50% of teaching hours at month 6, ≤40% at month 12, ≤30% at month 18, ≤15% at month 24, ≤10% at month 36, each step conditional on trainer-led batches matching founder-led ones. The slower month-12 step costs nothing in Year 1 (trainer headcount is the same either way) [EST].

Unit economics at the full price: an L3 seat contributes ₹50.4k before acquisition cost and a 12-seat batch breaks even at 3.6 students; at the step-1 price ₹39.6k and 38% of a batch; at today's assumed fee ₹22.2k and 8 students [EST]. The business breaks even at about 6.5 career enrolments a month at the month-12 cost base on the step-1 fee (5.5 at the full fee) [EST]. The founder's unpaid teaching is today's margin: replacing it costs about ₹10L in Year 1 and ₹17–19L a year thereafter, plus about ₹8L a year for an academy manager from Year 2 [EST]. GST treatment is worth about ₹12.7L a year and needs a chartered accountant's opinion.

## Investment: ₹30 lakh planned, ₹35 lakh authorised, released in two tranches [REC]

- **Tranche 1, authorise now: up to ₹15L** (expected use about ₹10.9L). Admissions counsellor (month 1), lead trainer (month 2–3), content and video, CRM/WhatsApp, website, legal/accounting/B&WSSC, test-level advertising (≤₹60k/month), basic recording of theory (₹1L), a weekend/evening batch slot in the studio from month 5. No new premises. A Conservative trajectory uses ₹13.4L by month 9, so the Tranche-1 review is held no later than month 9.
- **Tranche 2, release at gate G1 (decide at month 6; month 9 at the latest): up to ₹15L more.** Dedicated ~1,300 sq ft space the month after G1 (fit-out ₹10.4L, deposit ₹3.1L, stations ₹1.2L), second trainer, advertising to ₹1.0L/month, two regional pop-up pilots, price step 1.
- **Contingency: ₹5L above the ₹30L**, because the Staged Plan's peak cash of ₹28.8L leaves only ₹1.2L of headroom and the model's cash excludes tax, GST timing and any founder draw. It is drawn only on the founder's sign-off, only for timing slippage, never for new scope. The recorded hybrid pre-work library (₹3.5L) is deferred to Year 2; the Destination pilot runs on live-online pre-work.
- **The ₹35–50L band is not a Year-1 budget.** It is reserved for the regional engine in Year 2 (₹4–10L) and released only at gate G2. The ₹50L plan is rejected for Year 1: at Base rates it yields EBITDA of −₹3.0L and a ₹44.2L cash need [EST].
- **If G1 is missed,** spending stops at Tranche 1 and PNA runs as a profitable boutique (₹10L plan at Base rates: +₹10.8L EBITDA on ₹39.8L revenue, about 52 career students a year) while the cause is fixed. If G1 is still missed at month 12, the step-back timeline is abandoned and the regional engine is dropped.

## The biggest risks

Price realisation (proof arrives late or weak, and the family's all-in cost rises by about 45% at the full price); the founder step-back hurting conversion or quality; cash against the ₹30L line (₹1.2L of headroom; Conservative needs ₹14.9L against ₹10L); optimistic funnel benchmarks (the cost range for the same enrolments is ~9x wide, none of it makeup-specific, and the plan already runs amber on acquisition cost); capacity binding before demand (seats bind at month 6 in every run; today's space holds about 58 flagship starts a year against a plan needing 153%); GST; an out-of-town safety incident; and misleading-claim exposure if "100% placement"-style promises creep in.

## Decisions needed from the founder now

1. Supply actual enrolments, fees, enquiry log, studio stations and team costs; every number above changes when they arrive.
2. Choose: a profitable founder-taught boutique (₹10L) or a transferable institution (₹30L staged, a loss of about ₹7L after your own salary in Year 1, ₹33L of profit after salary by Year 3). The staged plan starts as the first and becomes the second at G1.
3. Authorise Tranche 1 (₹15L), the release rules, and the ₹30L-plus-₹5L-contingency envelope.
4. Decide the studio's future and accept that it stays a separate business (the academy's proof and practice engine, not its revenue line).
5. Hire an admissions counsellor and a lead trainer at market pay, and stop being the inbox.
6. Agree the step-back path and a ₹75k/month founder-role salary in the model.
7. Accept published fees (with the all-in figure), EMI and refund terms, outcomes published as distributions, and no placement or income promises.
8. Engage a chartered accountant on GST, and hand over Instagram, GBP and website access.
