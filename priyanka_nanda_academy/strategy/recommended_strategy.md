## 1. Executive recommendation [REC]

1. **Build one thing well:** a trainer-delivered, small-batch, bridal-specialist **Chandigarh Flagship** with a published course ladder, a counsellor-run WhatsApp funnel and verifiable alumni outcomes. Not a second site, not a marketplace, not a national online course.
2. **The first rupee buys proof and response discipline, not advertising:** Google Business Profile, an academy Instagram, a CRM with a sub-5-minute reply standard, portfolio-day shoots and an Alumni Outcomes Report. PNA is invisible for every non-branded search tested today (CA-07 [FACT of absence]).
3. **Raise the flagship price only after proof exists, in two steps** (₹75k list, then ₹90k list; ₹80k realised is the planning fee). Price is the single biggest lever: a ₹23.3L swing on Year-1 EBITDA [EST, Base].
4. **Fund the ₹30L Base plan, but staged:** authorise up to ₹15L now (Tranche 1, through gate G1); release the balance up to ₹30L only when G1 is green (dedicated space, second trainer, hybrid content, media to ₹1.0L/month). The ₹30–50L band stays unreleased for Year 2 and only against G2. The ₹50L plan is rejected for Year 1.
5. **The founder steps back by proof, not by calendar:** ≤50% of student contact hours at M6, ≤40% at M12, ≤30% at M18, ≤15% at M24, ≤10% at M36, each step conditional on trainer-led cohorts matching founder-led ones (G1 metrics).
6. **The second engine is regional destination learning:** students from Punjab, HP, Haryana and J&K come to Chandigarh for the Destination format. It is released at G2 in Year 2 after two cheap pilots. A partner-hosted Ludhiana satellite waits for G3a; franchise or licensing waits for G3b.
7. **Plan outcome (Base, gated) [EST]:** Year-1 revenue ₹71.8L ex-GST and EBITDA +₹6.0L before any founder salary (−₹3.0L after ₹75k/month); Year-3 revenue ₹166.9L and EBITDA ₹44.9L before founder salary; Year-3 training revenue ₹1.89 cr incl. GST.
8. **The honest downside:** at Conservative rates the academy loses money in all three years (−₹13.1L / −₹7.2L / −₹9.6L) and needs ₹15L of cash against a ₹10L plan. The gates and stop-loss rules exist so that this is caught by M9, not M36.
9. **Before any of this, the founder supplies her actual enrolments, fees, enquiry log and studio capacity.** Every PNA number in this project is an assumption until then.
10. **The founder must choose:** a profitable founder-taught boutique (₹10L plan: about +₹10.7L EBITDA on ₹39.8L revenue, ~52 career students a year, she keeps teaching) or a transferable institution (₹30L staged: about break-even after her salary in Year 1, step-back possible, EBITDA ₹17.6L / ₹44.9L in Years 2–3 before founder salary). The staged plan starts as the first and becomes the second at G1.

### Q2. Is Chandigarh alone an adequate market? Can it attract students from surrounding areas?

**Enough to start; not enough to finish.** At a 15–25% share, tricity tuition alone supports roughly ₹0.5–0.8 cr for one centre [EST, methodology §2]. The Base plan reaches 13% of tricity makeup-led learners by Year 3, inside the plausible single-centre band, and its revenue share runs ahead of its learner share because PNA's realised fee is about 1.8x the tricity average. Growth beyond ₹0.8 cr has to come from out-of-town students (30% of career enrolments by Year 3 in Base), working-artist masterclasses, and later the hybrid and online layers.

**H1 is supported at the ceiling but is not the binding constraint for 18–24 months.** Capacity (seats bind from month 6 in the Base plan), visibility (3/10) and founder hours bind first.

**H2 (destination hub) is plausible, unproven.** For: Chandigarh's female higher-education GER is 75.4 against 28.5 for India, consistent with student inflow [FACT, S-CHD-11]; the four-state pool of women aged 17–35 is 26x the tricity [EST, S-CHD §8.3]. Against: no origin data exists for beauty students; PG cost (₹8–17.5k/month [FACT, S-CHD-20]) and safety (1 of 11 sampled PG listings girls-only) are real parent objections; "no training centre nearby" is a top barrier nationally [FACT, S-IND-01]; Orane is present in every Punjab city [CLAIM, S-BM-15]. **Test it for almost nothing:** tag every lead by city from M1; the G2 threshold is ≥20% of qualified leads and ≥15% of enrolments from out of town before any regional spend.

### Q4. Should scale come from one flagship, multi-centre, online, hybrid, a marketplace, or adjacent streams?

**One flagship first; destination learning second; everything else behind gates or rejected.** The model's own unit economics decide it (BM_Unit_Economics, Base, directional): an owned Ludhiana centre loses about ₹7.4L a year at 46 students and breaks even at 62, which is 11–21% of Ludhiana's estimated resident paid learners in a chain-served city; a full marketplace loses ₹932 per booking before fixed costs with LTV/CAC of 0.35; a franchise earns about ₹2L a year per unit after support; destination students and a partner-hosted satellite are positive at small scale. See §4.

### Q5. What should the brand stand for and whom should it target?

**"The small-batch bridal makeup academy inside a working bridal studio."** Students learn the **PNA Method** on real faces, assist on real bridal bookings in season, and leave with a portfolio; fees, practice hours and alumni outcomes are published so families can check before paying. Trust moves from the founder to three assets the business owns: the PNA Method, PNA Certified Educators, and published PNA Alumni outcomes. Keep the founder's name as the masterbrand; no rename.

Targets, in order: **S1** aspiring professional artists aged 18–25 in the tricity (core revenue); **S3** working artists and salon staff (profit, repeat purchase, future trainers); **S7** out-of-town students as an overlay (the growth bet); **S2** career-switchers and homemakers 25–40 (volume, flexible format); **S5** self-makeup learners (seasonal cash, awareness). Parents and spouses are the decision unit and get their own message track. Defer S4 government-scheme skilling.

### Q6. What is the correct admissions funnel and channel mix?

WhatsApp is the rail, not the funnel. For career programmes: Meta click-to-WhatsApp or Google Search → counsellor reply within 5 minutes → **campus visit with a parent** (or a ₹999 paid taster for S2) → token → EMI. Masterclasses and self-makeup are self-serve. The website is the trust hub, not the closer. Steady-state route mix target: CTWA 30% · Search 20% · Organic 20% · Referral 20% · Taster 10% [HYP].

Paid media stays at test level (₹40–60k/month) until the five spend gates (SG1–SG5) are green for four consecutive weeks; the Base plan then ramps to ₹1.0L/month by May 2027. Speed-to-lead is the cheapest lever: missing the 5-minute standard costs about ₹32k/month of extra media for the same enrolments, or 2.3 enrolments a month at the same budget (Base) [EST]. The founder must not be the inbox.

## 4. The recommended model and why not the alternatives

### 4.1 Primary model: the Chandigarh Flagship [REC]

One Chandigarh academy, run as a trainer-delivered, small-batch (≤12), bridal-specialist school on a codified PNA Method, sold through a published ladder (L1 Taster → L2 Foundation → L3 Pro → L4 Masterclasses → L5 Pro Circle), closed by a counsellor at a parent visit, financed by EMI, and integrated with the founder's studio for live-bride practice and proof. The Business Model agent called this the "Hybrid Chandigarh Flagship" and I have renamed it (see §10, overrule 1): in Year 1 the hybrid element is a feature (recorded theory pre-work and the Destination format for out-of-town students), not the model.

**Why this and not the status quo:** the status quo fails the founder's goal (K2) and leaves the brand invisible while local rivals keep improving their funnels. **Why this and not a bigger bet:** it is the necessary foundation for every other option, it scores highest among full-scale models under all three weightings in the BM scorecard (3.85 / 3.90 / 3.85), and its economics are the only ones in the model that are positive at today's scale once the price step lands (cohort break-even 3.6 seats of 12).

### 4.2 Second engine: Regional Destination Engine [REC, released at G2]

Compressed Destination-format courses in Chandigarh (4 weeks online pre-work + 5–6 weeks full-day intensive) for students from Punjab, HP, Haryana and J&K, with vetted women-only accommodation at pass-through prices, a parents' online orientation, regional alumni ambassadors and a rotating pop-up circuit (Ludhiana, Patiala, Jalandhar, Amritsar, Shimla). It cuts an out-of-town student's stay from about 12 weeks to about 6 and her living cost from ₹33–54k to ₹17–28k [EST, A-BM-12]. Each incremental destination student adds contribution with no new rent while flagship seats are spare, which is why it dominates an owned regional centre on the model's indifference rules (an owned Ludhiana centre only wins above about 69 local students when 10 would travel; a partner satellite needs more than 2.4x the destination number) [EST, methodology §3 #9].

Two pilots of ≤₹2L in total may run after G1 (from about M9). Scale only at G2.
