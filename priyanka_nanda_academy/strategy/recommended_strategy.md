## 1. Executive recommendation [REC]

1. **Build one thing well:** a trainer-delivered, small-batch, bridal-specialist **Chandigarh Flagship** with a published course ladder, a counsellor-run WhatsApp funnel and verifiable alumni outcomes. Not a second site, not a marketplace, not a national online course. The independent review confirms the direction; what it corrected was the money, the gate and the ownership.
2. **The first rupee buys proof and response discipline, not advertising:** Google Business Profile, an academy Instagram, a CRM with a sub-5-minute reply standard, portfolio-day shoots and an Alumni Outcomes Report. PNA was invisible for every non-branded search we could run, but Instagram, Maps and Justdial were blocked in our environment, so the founder verifies her real footprint before the first meeting (CA-07; RT-09).
3. **Raise the flagship price only after proof exists, in two steps** (₹75k list the month after G1, then ₹90k list; ₹80k realised is the planning fee). Price is the biggest and the least-tested lever: a ₹19.7L swing on planning-case Year-1 EBITDA, zero price elasticity in the model, no willingness-to-pay evidence yet. Step 2 stops adding value if it costs about 21% of career conversion.
4. **Plan to need ₹25L, authorise ₹35L, and know the worst case: ≈₹36L if nobody acts (above the ceiling), ≈₹25L with the mandatory March cut, ≈₹33L if the post-move season fails.** Tranche 1 (up to ₹15L; ₹11.8L expected) runs to a G1 decision at the end of July 2027; Tranche 2 (up to ₹30L cumulative) releases the space, the media ramp and the second counsellor only on that evidence; a ₹5L contingency sits above. The ceiling holds only with three hard rules: a mandatory cut at the March 2027 review if the funnel fails (KT-1a), no lease until G1 is green on May–July actuals, and a post-move stop-loss (KT-10). Worst plausible loss: **about ₹36L by October 2027 if nobody acts (visible February 2027)**; **about ₹25L (₹25.4L) with the mandatory March cut**; **about ₹33L if G1 passes on a false positive and the peak season fails (visible September 2027)**, of which ₹11.6L is sunk fit-out and ₹3.1L deposit is recoverable.
5. **Someone other than the founder runs it.** An **Admissions & Operations Lead** is hired in month 1 (counsellor pay plus a ₹15k/month premium) to own the 90-day plan and chair the weekly funnel review; the founder chairs the Friday plan review and the gate decisions. The founder's teaching load is disclosed, not hidden: in the planning case it peaks at about 114 hours a month in August 2027 (about 128 in May if G1 comes early at M6) and stays above 106 through October, with a second trainer from August and written cover for her absence and for Lead-Trainer attrition.
6. **The founder steps back by proof, not by calendar:** ≤50% of student contact hours at M6, ≤40% at M12, ≤30% at M18, ≤15% at M24, ≤10% at M36, each step conditional on trainer-led cohorts matching founder-led ones; if her teaching exceeds 110 hours a month for two consecutive months, the second trainer is pulled forward and the share target is held at ≤50% until the hire lands.
7. **The second engine is regional destination learning:** students from Punjab, HP, Haryana and J&K come to Chandigarh for the Destination format; a six-student pilot runs in the August–September 2027 intakes; scale is released at G2 in Year 2. A partner-hosted Ludhiana satellite waits for G3a; franchise or licensing waits for G3b.
8. **Plan outcome (planning case, G1 at M9, all gates passed) [EST]:** Year-1 revenue ₹60.6L ex-GST, EBITDA −₹2.8L before any founder salary (−₹11.8L after ₹75k/month), 81 career enrolments, peak cash ₹25.2L (₹32.6L if the founder also draws her salary from the academy). Year-3 revenue ₹162.9L and EBITDA ₹39.3L before founder salary (₹28.8L after), but **33% of that Year-3 revenue (₹54.1L) sits behind price step 2 and gate G2**; without them the flagship earns ₹108.8L and roughly zero EBITDA. Payback on the founder's cash is month 29 before her salary (IRR 46% to M36) and month 40 after it (IRR −10% to M36, 33% to M60).
9. **Before any of this, the founder supplies her actual enrolments, fees, enquiry log and studio capacity.** Every PNA number in this project is an assumption until then.
10. **The founder must choose, with eyes open:** a profitable founder-taught boutique (₹10L plan: about +₹10.8L EBITDA a year before her salary, about +₹1.8L after it, on ₹39.8L revenue, peak cash ₹3.1L, ~52 career students, she keeps teaching) or a transferable institution (₹35L authorised: about −₹2.8L EBITDA before her salary and about −₹11.8L after it in Year 1, cash back only in Year 3, and a bet on two things the model cannot yet evidence: that families will pay ₹90k for proven outcomes, and that out-of-town students will come). The staged plan lets her find out for about ₹12L before committing ₹14.7L to a lease.

### Q2. Is Chandigarh alone an adequate market? Can it attract students from surrounding areas?

**Enough to start; not enough to finish.** At a 15–25% share, tricity tuition alone supports roughly ₹0.5–0.8 cr for one centre [EST, methodology §2]. The plan reaches 13% of all tricity makeup-led learners by Year 3, but about 20% of the professional-programme tier it actually competes in [EST, reviewer], at a realised fee about 1.8x the tricity average. **H1 therefore binds at the plan's fee by Year 3 unless the out-of-town share is delivered** (30% of career enrolments by Year 3). Growth beyond ₹0.8 cr has to come from out-of-town students, working-artist masterclasses, and later the hybrid and online layers.

**For 12–18 months H1 is not the binding constraint.** Capacity (seats bind in the studio from month 7 in the planning case, through the May–July peak), visibility, founder hours and the quality of execution bind first.

**H2 (destination hub) is plausible, unproven.** For: Chandigarh's female higher-education GER is 75.4 against 28.5 for India, consistent with student inflow [FACT, S-CHD-11]; the four-state pool of women aged 17–35 is 26x the tricity [EST, S-CHD §8.3]. Against: no origin data exists for beauty students; PG cost (₹8–17.5k/month [FACT, S-CHD-20]) and safety (1 of 11 sampled PG listings girls-only) are real parent objections; "no training centre nearby" is a top barrier nationally [FACT, S-IND-01]; Orane is present in every Punjab city [CLAIM, S-BM-15]. **Test it for almost nothing:** tag every lead by city from M1; run the six-student Destination pilot in the August–September 2027 intakes; the G2 threshold is ≥20% of qualified leads and ≥15% of enrolments from out of town before any regional spend.

**What this means for PNA:** fee opacity is the norm (only Zuri publishes locally; 4 of 10 nationally), nobody publishes cohort outcomes, and every serious player runs counsellor-led conversion with financing. ### Q4. Should scale come from one flagship, multi-centre, online, hybrid, a marketplace, or adjacent streams?

**One flagship first; destination learning second; everything else behind gates or rejected.** The model's own unit economics decide it (BM_Unit_Economics, Base, directional): an owned Ludhiana centre loses about ₹7.4L a year at 46 students and breaks even at 62, which is 11–21% of Ludhiana's estimated resident paid learners in a chain-served city; a full marketplace loses ₹932 per booking before fixed costs with LTV/CAC of 0.35; a franchise earns about ₹2L a year per unit after support; destination students and a partner-hosted satellite are positive at small scale. See §4.

### Q5. What should the brand stand for and whom should it target?

**"The small-batch bridal makeup academy inside a working bridal studio."** Students learn the **PNA Method** on real faces, assist on real bridal bookings in season, and leave with a portfolio; fees, practice hours and alumni outcomes are published so families can check before paying. Trust moves from the founder to three assets the business owns: the PNA Method, PNA Certified Educators, and published PNA Alumni outcomes. Keep the founder's name as the masterbrand; no rename.

Targets, in order: **S1** aspiring professional artists aged 18–25 in the tricity (core revenue); **S3** working artists and salon staff (profit, repeat purchase, future trainers; the Year-1 masterclass volume is unevidenced until Masterclass #1 sells); **S7** out-of-town students as an overlay (the growth bet); **S2** career-switchers and homemakers 25–40 (volume, flexible format); **S5** self-makeup learners (seasonal cash, awareness). Parents and spouses are the decision unit and get their own message track. Defer S4 government-scheme skilling.

### Q6. What is the correct admissions funnel and channel mix?

WhatsApp is the rail, not the funnel. For career programmes: Meta click-to-WhatsApp or Google Search → counsellor reply within 5 minutes → **campus visit with a parent** (or a ₹999 paid taster for S2) → token → EMI. Masterclasses and self-makeup are self-serve. The website is the trust hub, not the closer. Steady-state route mix target: CTWA 30% · Search 20% · Organic 20% · Referral 20% · Taster 10% [HYP].

Paid media stays at test level (≤₹15k/month until G0, ≤₹60k/month until G1) and ramps only after G1 (₹80k in August and ₹1.0L/month from September 2027 in the planning case: ₹7.0L of paid media in Year 1). Two honest caveats. First, Year 1 is acquisition-inefficient by design: the growth team and tools cost about 1.5x the paid media because they are hired ahead of volume to build proof, so SG4 reads amber on media CAC (₹11.7k) and **red on fully loaded acquisition (53% of career revenue) in months 7–12** of the planning case; SG4 green (≤40%) is a Year-2 expectation. Second, the ₹1.0L/month steady state is about 1.9x what the route-level funnel says buys the same enrolments (₹37.6k); the gap is awareness and retargeting spend, reconciled in the model by a calibration factor (A-ECO-51 = 2.42), not by evidence. A ₹70k/month steady state is a legitimate founder choice: it costs about 7 enrolments and ₹0.5L of EBITDA in the planning case. The first paid tests (January–March 2027) replace the calibration with measured CAC.

Speed-to-lead is cheap and probably valuable: the model says missing the 5-minute standard costs about ₹32k/month of extra media for the same enrolments [EST], but that figure is the output of a vendor-benchmark input (A-GRO-24), so **H7 is plausible, untested**; the SG2 discipline costs nothing, so it stays. The founder must not be the inbox.

## 4. The recommended model and why not the alternatives

### 4.1 Primary model: the Chandigarh Flagship [REC]

One Chandigarh academy, run as a trainer-delivered, small-batch (≤12), bridal-specialist school on a codified PNA Method, sold through a published ladder (L1 Taster → L2 Foundation → L3 Pro → L4 Masterclasses → L5 Pro Circle), closed by a counsellor at a parent visit, financed by EMI, and integrated with the founder's studio for live-bride practice and proof. The Business Model agent called this the "Hybrid Chandigarh Flagship" and I have renamed it (see §10, overrule 1): in Year 1 the hybrid element is a feature (live-online theory pre-work and the Destination format for out-of-town students), not the model.

**Why this and not the status quo:** the status quo fails the founder's goal (KO-2) and leaves the brand invisible while local rivals keep improving their funnels. **Why this and not a bigger bet:** it is the necessary foundation for every other option, it scores highest among full-scale models under all three weightings in the BM scorecard (3.85 / 3.90 / 3.85), and its economics are the only ones in the model that are positive at today's scale once the price step lands (cohort break-even 3.6 seats of 12 at the full fee; 38% of a cohort at the step-1 fee).

### 4.2 Second engine: Regional Destination Engine [REC, pilot at M10–M11, released at G2]

Compressed Destination-format courses in Chandigarh (4 weeks online pre-work + 5–6 weeks full-day intensive) for students from Punjab, HP, Haryana and J&K, with vetted women-only accommodation at pass-through prices, a parents' online orientation, regional alumni ambassadors and a rotating pop-up circuit (Ludhiana, Patiala, Jalandhar, Amritsar, Shimla). It cuts an out-of-town student's stay from about 12 weeks to about 6 and her living cost from ₹33–54k to ₹17–28k [EST, A-BM-12]. Each incremental destination student adds contribution with no new rent while flagship seats are spare, which is why it dominates an owned regional centre on the model's indifference rules (an owned Ludhiana centre only wins above about 69 local students when 10 would travel; a partner satellite needs more than 2.4x the destination number) [EST, methodology §3 #9].

The pilot is **six out-of-town L3 students across the August and September 2027 intakes (M10–M11)**, on live-online pre-work, after G1 and after the move; it adds about three students and ₹0.6L of revenue to the planning case. Two pop-up pilots of ≤₹2L in total may run after G1. Scale only at G2.

This is the honest shape of the institution path: a three-year payback on a key-person business with a lease, in exchange for a business that can run without her.
