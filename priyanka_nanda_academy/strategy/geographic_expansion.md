## 0. Summary

1. **Stage 1 (M0–18): win the tricity, structurally.** A hybrid flagship in Chandigarh with trainers, a published fee ladder, CRM, and a studio practicum (companion file §6.1). PNA holds about 4% of a ₹5.7 crore, 1,256-student base market (CA-05). Headroom is 3–5x before H1 binds.
2. **Stage 2 (M12–30, released at Gate G2): regional destination learning, not new centres.** Students from Punjab, HP, Haryana and J&K come to Chandigarh for compressed hybrid intensives (5–6 weeks in the city instead of about 12). Accommodation is vetted and women-only, and parents get an online orientation. A rotating pop-up circuit runs in Ludhiana, Jalandhar, Patiala, Amritsar and Shimla. Stage 2b (after G3a) is a **partner-hosted satellite in Ludhiana**, not an owned centre.
3. **Stage 3 (M30–48+): national reach through online/hybrid**, with licensing or franchising only after G3b. Delhi NCR stays online/hybrid plus occasional founder masterclasses. There is no owned NCR centre in the 3-year horizon.
4. **Centre vs destination [EST]:**
   - An owned Ludhiana centre costs about ₹29 lakh a year in fixed costs. It breaks even at about 46 professional students, 9–15% of Ludhiana's estimated resident paid market, against Orane, Lakmé, VLCC, Headmasters and Knuckout.
   - The same 46 students pulled to Chandigarh add about **₹26 lakh of contribution** with no new fixed cost, while flagship seats are spare.
   - An owned centre only beats destination learning if it enrols about **46 + 0.9 × (students who would have come to Chandigarh anyway)**. For example, it needs more than 64 local enrolments if 20 would have travelled.
   - A partner-hosted satellite beats destination only if it enrols about **1.7x** the students destination would.
5. **The student's side is the real constraint.** A 12-week offline course adds ₹33–54k of living cost (+41–68% on an ₹80k fee). The hybrid intensive cuts that to ₹17–28k (+21–35%) (A-BM-12; S-CHD-20/21). Parents' safety concerns are documented (only 1 of 11 sampled PG listings was girls-only; "only 25 licensed" PGs claim; fatal PG fire) (S-CHD-18/20).
6. **Delhi NCR: avoid physically.** It has 7.4x the tricity pool and 2.43x income. But rent is 3.5x, premium competitors publish ₹2.25–5.95 lakh prices, a ₹35k discount floor exists, and the bridal median price is the same ₹25k as Chandigarh, so graduates earn no more there (S-CHD-12/22; S-NC-22/26/31).
7. **Measurable gates** (section 6) govern each step: fill and utilisation, CAC payback, completion and NPS, trainer bench, founder share of teaching, out-of-town share, and pop-up conversion. **Kill triggers** stop failing pilots early.

## 1. Stage logic

| | **Stage 1: Tricity flagship** | **Stage 2: Regional destination learning (2a); partner-hosted satellite (2b)** | **Stage 3: National reach** |
|---|---|---|---|

| Objective | Turn on-demand founder classes into a trainer-delivered academy that fills cohorts and proves outcomes | Escape the tricity ceiling (H1) at the lowest capex; test and then exploit H2 | Monetise a codified method and brand beyond the North |
| Geography | Chandigarh flagship. | Out of scope | Any second site | Owned centres; franchising | Owned NCR campus (unless re-justified) |
| Indicative investment | ₹10 / 27–39 / 42–50 lakh (companion §6.5) | ₹4–10 lakh (2a); ₹2–5 lakh per partner site (2b) | Funded from operating cash flow |
| Exit gate | **G1** (flagship proven), then **G2** | **G3a** for 2b; **G3b** for Stage 3 | — |

**Why this order.**

- **Fix the cheapest constraint first.** The binding constraints today are visibility (digital maturity 3/10, S-LC-51), response discipline (H7) and founder hours (H3), not market size. They cost little to fix and every later stage depends on them.
- **Grow regionally without new fixed cost.** Destination learning adds students at the margin, with no new rent or site staff, while flagship seats are spare (section 4).
- **Add a site only once the evidence demands it.** A physical site follows only when one city shows demand that converts locally but is blocked by distance. A partner-hosted satellite tests that before owned capex. Mao Geping took 24 years to reach 9 campuses (S-INT-01), and Aveda moved to a licensed-operator model (S-INT-32).

## 3. Reading the table

### 3.1 Tricity (Stage 1)

- **Size.** The base market is about 1,256 paid learners and ₹5.7 crore a year. That supports a ₹0.6–1.1 crore flagship at 10–20% share, and the base plan needs about 8% (companion §6.1).
- **Location within the tricity.** The Sector 34A cluster is where chains sit and where generic SEO pages point (S-LC §5). PNA's Sector 8 studio location supports a *different* proposition: a boutique studio-academy with live brides. Do not move into Sector 34, where rent is ₹113–130/sq ft vs a ₹47 city median (S-CHD-22).
- **Uncovered demand.** Panchkula has no visible dedicated academy (S-LC §7 #5). Weekend batches and geo-targeted CTWA can test it at near-zero cost.

### 3.2 Regional North India (Stage 2): explicit assessment

**For:**

- The Punjab, Haryana, HP and J&K pool is 12.4M women aged 17–35, 26x the tricity (S-CHD §8.3). The implied outstation draw today is 1 in 39,000, so small reach gains add real volume.
- Bridal prices in Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar (₹21–25k) match Chandigarh's, so a graduate's earning potential at home is not lower (S-CHD-13).
- North India holds more than 30% of B&W employment. Punjab, J&K and HP are named sourcing clusters for the sector (S-IND-01 pp.33–35).
- 92% of surveyed youth want to work in their home state (S-IND-01 p.55). That fits a "train in Chandigarh, work at home" proposition backed by home-city alumni referrals.

**Against:**

- Punjab's income is India-average (1.03x) and J&K's is below (0.74x) (S-CHD-06).
- Female work participation is low in Punjab (28.0%) and Haryana (22.8%), so family permission and mobility limit outstation study (S-CHD-09).
- Living costs and safety are real barriers (A-BM-12; S-CHD-18/20).
- "No training centre nearby" is a top barrier to training (S-IND-01 p.57). That argues for *taking the discovery step to the student* (pop-ups) even if the main course stays in Chandigarh.
- Orane is present across the region (Ludhiana, Jalandhar, Amritsar, Jammu, Shimla, Solan), and Lakmé is in Ludhiana and Shimla (S-LC §3; S-BM-15).
- Punjabi-language support is needed outside the tricity (S-CHD §7).

**Verdict [REC]:** treat the region as a **feeder market for a Chandigarh destination proposition**, reached by pop-ups and hybrid. Promote Ludhiana to a partner-hosted site only if its gate metrics show distance-blocked demand.

### 3.3 Delhi NCR: explicit assessment

| Factor | Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Pool and income | 3.46M women aged 17–35 (NCT), 7.4x tricity; 2.43x India income (S-CHD §2) | Largest and richest addressable pool in the North |
| Graduate economics | Bridal median ₹25k NCR, ₹18–20k Gurgaon/Noida, the same as or below Chandigarh (S-CHD-13) | No ROI premium for the student; price premiums come from brand access (DBA, Pearl), not market rates |
| Competition | Published premium prices DBA ₹2.25–5.95 lakh and Pearl ₹4.45 lakh; chains; Meribindiya's full funnel stack; Jawed Habib ₹35k discount floor; 1,438+ UrbanPro listings (S-NC §3, §7) | A squeezed middle for an unknown regional founder brand |
| Cost | Rent ~₹167/sq ft Delhi, ₹94–105 Noida/Gurgaon. A 1,200 sq ft space costs about ₹2.0 lakh/month vs ₹56k in Chandigarh (S-CHD §6) | Doubles to triples the fixed cost per seat |
| Founder access | 4–5 h by road | Founder-led masterclasses possible occasionally; daily presence impossible |
| Flow direction | Delhi academies plausibly *pull* premium students from the region [HYP, S-CHD §7] | NCR is more of a competitive threat to PNA's catchment than an opportunity |

**Verdict: avoid physical presence for at least 3 years.** In Stage 3, use NCR as a market for (a) the national online foundation module, (b) occasional founder masterclass pop-ups for working artists, and (c) a licensed partner only after G3b. Revisit if ≥10% of qualified leads come organically from NCR.

## 4. Centre economics vs pulling out-of-town students to Chandigarh

### 4.1 Directional comparison for one feeder city (Ludhiana; P1 students at ₹80k realised)

| Route | Upfront capex to PNA | Annual fixed cost to PNA | Contribution per P1 student to PNA | Illustration: 46 Ludhiana-origin P1 students a year | Quality control | Founder-brand exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Destination learning** (Chandigarh hybrid intensive + pop-ups + accommodation partner) | ₹4–8 lakh (pre-work content shared with flagship; pop-up kit) | Pop-up circuit about ₹5–8 lakh gross, 60–80% recovered by tickets. Discovery events net about −₹9k each; masterclass pop-ups about +₹43–53k each | **≈ ₹56k** (₹80k − ₹6k consumables − ₹3.2k EMI/fees − ₹12.5k CAC − ₹2k accommodation coordination). ≈ ₹46k if a new batch slot is needed | **≈ +₹26 lakh** contribution; no new site | Highest (one site, studio practicum) | Concentrated: founder masterclasses, live studio |
| **(b) Partner-hosted satellite** (PNA trainer teaches in a non-competing salon's training room; 20–30% revenue share; A-BM-19) | ₹2–5 lakh (stations, branding, kit) | Low (trainer travel/stay) | **≈ ₹33k** (75% retained − variable − ₹10k trainer allocation) | ≈ +₹15 lakh | Medium (partner premises) | Diluted: no studio practicum locally |
| **(c) Owned centre** (1,200 sq ft, lead trainer + trainer + counsellor) | **₹13–20 lakh** + runway | **≈ ₹29 lakh** (A-BM-13) | ≈ ₹63k before fixed costs | **≈ ₹0** (breakeven at about 46) | Medium-high | Diluted; needs a site lead and a GM |
| **(d) Franchise** (15–20% royalty; brand fee about ₹4.3–5.0 lakh) | ₹5–10 lakh to build the system (manuals, training, audits, legal) | Support about ₹3–5 lakh per unit early on | ₹12–16k royalty | ≈ +₹5.5–7.4 lakh royalty (+ about ₹1 lakh on short courses) less support | Lowest | Highest risk of dilution |

Arithmetic: A-BM-13/15/16/19; benchmark royalties from S-BM-08/09. All [EST].

**Indifference rules [EST]** (who wins, given how many students each route actually enrols):

- **Owned centre vs destination:** an owned Ludhiana centre beats destination only if local enrolments exceed **(₹29.1 lakh + N_dest × ₹56.3k) ÷ ₹62.8k**:
  - more than 46 if no one would travel;
  - more than 55 if 10 would travel;
  - more than 64 if 20 would travel.

  55–65 students is **11–21% of Ludhiana's estimated 307–512 resident paid learners**, a high bar for a new brand in a chain-served city.
- **Partner-hosted vs destination:** a partner-hosted satellite beats destination if it enrols **more than 1.7x** the students who would come to Chandigarh. For example, 17+ if 10 would travel, or 34+ if 20 would.
- **Franchise:** it never wins on contribution at 1–3 units. Its case is reach without capital, available only after G3b.

**Implication.** Destination learning dominates **while flagship seats are spare** and distance does not suppress conversion much. The empirical question, answered by pop-up and CRM data, is **how much a local site lifts enrolment**. Only a lift of about 1.7x or more justifies a partner-hosted site, and about 3x or more an owned one.

- Make the hybrid intensive the *default* for anyone beyond about 2 hours.
- Run compressed or weekend timetables for commuter cities.
- Negotiate cohort rates with accommodation partners.
- Consider an "outstation bursary" of ₹5–10k funded from the lower pop-up CAC rather than discounting the course (Economics to test elasticity, M4).

## 6. Decision gates and triggers

Measure every metric from the CRM, attendance and finance records defined in `research/customer_segments.md` §8. Review gates monthly in a 30-minute funnel review, and formally at each gate. "All must hold" unless stated.

**Definitions:**

- **Utilisation:** enrolled seats ÷ offered seats (batch cap 12) across cohorts in the period.
- **CAC payback (months):** blended CAC ÷ monthly cash contribution per student over the payment/EMI schedule.
- **Founder share:** founder-delivered student contact hours ÷ total contact hours.
- **NPS:** end-of-course survey.
- **Completion:** ≥80% attendance + assessment passed + portfolio produced.

| Gate | Unlocks | Earliest | Metric thresholds (measurement window) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G0: Instrumented baseline** | Stage-1 spend beyond about ₹8–10 lakh (trainer hire, content, paid media tests) | M3 | Founder data pull complete (12–24 months of enrolments, fees, enquiry log). CRM + WhatsApp BSP live with **≥95% of leads source- and city-tagged**. **Median first response ≤15 min** in working hours (target ≤5 min by M6). Fee ladder, course pages and GBP published. Lead trainer hired and shadow-teaching. B&WSSC fee-based enquiry lodged (S-BM-12) |
| **G1: Flagship proven** | Dedicated academy space (if needed); second trainer; counsellor; scaled paid media; first regional pilots (≤₹2 lakh) | M9–12 | **2 consecutive cohorts ≥75% fill.** Paid-social lead→enrolment **≥4%** (A-CUS-15). **CAC ≤12% of realised fee and CAC payback ≤3 months.** **Completion ≥85%**; **NPS ≥50**; ≥40% of completers post a review. ### 6.1 Kill and rollback triggers

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Flagship fill <50% for 2 consecutive cohorts | Freeze new fixed costs; review price, positioning and funnel speed before more media spend |
| After 3 trainer-led cohorts, NPS >15 points below founder-led, or team counselling conversion <60% of founder-led | Slow the founder step-back (hold founder share at ≤50%). Invest in trainer co-branding ("PNA Senior Educator") and signature-module video. Re-test after 2 cohorts |
| A pop-up city with <15 paid attendees twice, or attendee→P1 <3% after 3 events | Drop or rotate the city; move budget to the best-converting city |
| Hybrid pre-work completion <70%, or practical pass rate >10 points below offline | Revert that segment to the 12-week offline format; redesign pre-work as live online sessions (Airblack's live model, S-CUS-33) |
| Any serious safety incident involving an out-of-town student | Suspend that accommodation partner immediately; independent review before the next cohort |
| CAC payback >6 months for 2 quarters on any channel | Cut the channel; reallocate to referral/Search |
| Partner-hosted site <60% fill for 2 cohorts | Exit the partner arrangement (no lease exposure) |
