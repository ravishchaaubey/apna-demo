## 1. The one-paragraph answer

India's paid, makeup-led training market is worth about **₹388 cr a year** at base (range ₹80–1,373 cr) [EST], serving roughly 56,800 learners. No published figure for this market exists; the number is our own bottom-up build and its width reflects unobserved fee mix and the share of beauty learners who choose makeup. The tricity (Chandigarh, Mohali, Panchkula) is about **₹3.27 cr and ≈730 learners** a year [EST], 0.84% of India. The market a Chandigarh-led academy can realistically serve without opening a second site, the **SAM core**, is about **₹7.8 cr** (tricity + regional students who travel + hybrid-willing students + working-artist refresher training), with a further **₹7.9 cr online layer** that is not in the plan. PNA's planned Year-3 training revenue, the **SOM**, is **₹1.88 cr incl. GST** at base (range ₹0.41–3.83 cr) [EST]: 22% of the SAM core and 13% of tricity makeup-led learners. The strategic reading: the tricity alone supports a ₹0.5–0.8 cr single-centre academy; anything larger must pull students from Punjab, Haryana, HP and J&K and sell repeat training to working artists.

## 3. The market-sizing tree

```
TAM   India paid makeup-led training tuition .............. ₹388 cr   (80–1,373)   + online/short layer ₹22.5 cr (3–120)
 └─ [context only] 5-state North slice at 7.65% .........  ₹30 cr   (6–105)      KPMG workforce proxy; not used directly
     └─ SAM  serviceable by a Chandigarh-led academy ..... ₹15.7 cr  (3.0–78)
          ├─ SAM core ................................... ₹7.8 cr   (2.3–24)
          │   ├─ SAM-1 Tricity (reconciled) ............. ₹3.3 cr   (1.5–5.3)    ≈730 learners
          │   ├─ SAM-2 Regional destination, net ........ ₹1.9 cr   (0.2–10.6)   15% of PB/HR/HP/J&K learners reachable, less inflow already counted
          │   ├─ SAM-3 Hybrid beyond reach .............. ₹1.4 cr   (0.2–5.8)    8% hybrid-willing
          │   └─ SAM-4 Working-artist CPD ............... ₹1.2 cr   (0.4–2.5)    4,000 artists × 30% × ₹10k; scope extension (repeat buyers)
          └─ SAM-5 Online layer (North-reachable) ....... ₹7.9 cr   (0.8–54)
               └─ SOM  PNA Year-3 training revenue ...... ₹1.88 cr  (0.41–3.83)  = 22% of SAM core; 13% of tricity learners
                    Today (illustrative, NOT actual) .... ₹0.20 cr
```

**How each level is built (plain English):**

- **TAM** is the mean of two independent methods. Method 1 (pipeline) starts from the number of people certified by the sector skill council each year, scales up for the ~60% of training that is unorganised, keeps only self-paying learners, keeps only the share who choose makeup-led courses, and multiplies by a blended fee across four price tiers. Method 2 (supply) counts branded chain centres, multiplies by learners per centre, adds independents, and applies the same makeup share and fee. The two methods differ by about 2x at base (Method 1 alone gives ₹522 cr), which is why the band is wide.
- **The North slice (₹30 cr)** is shown only as a cross-check: Chandigarh, Punjab, Haryana, HP and J&K account for 7.65% of India's incremental beauty-workforce training need in the KPMG model [FACT, S-IND-01]. It is not used in the plan.
- **SAM core** is what one Chandigarh academy can serve: the tricity itself; regional learners within reasonable travel who would come to Chandigarh (15% reachable, net of the outstation students already counted in the tricity); learners further away who would accept a hybrid format (8%); and working artists buying masterclasses and refreshers (a scope extension, because they are repeat buyers rather than new entrants). A bottom-up five-state build (₹21.6 cr at tricity fees) and TAM × 7.65% restated at tricity fees (₹19.6 cr) agree within about 10%, which gives some comfort on the regional layer.
- **SAM online layer** is the national online/short-course layer × the share reachable with Hindi/Punjabi content. It is in the tree so that it is not forgotten, and out of the plan until PNA has an audience.
- **SOM** is PNA's Year-3 training revenue from the Base business forecast, grossed up for GST. It is a gated plan output, not a share-of-market assumption: the forecast is driven by the enrolment target and capped by capacity, then checked against the market for plausibility (13% of tricity makeup-led learners is inside the 10–30% single-centre band the Chandigarh research proposed).

## 4. Why the tricity number changed, and which one to use

The Chandigarh research file estimated tricity demand at ₹5.7 cr and about 1,256 students (CA-05). The India file's TAM implied a tricity share of only about 0.5%. The Economics workstream reconciled the two: CA-05 counts makeup **and** beauty-artistry learners at a diploma fee and includes outstation inflow, while the TAM counts makeup-led learners only. Of the 10.5x learner gap at base, 5.76x is explained by scope (1.43x), inflow (1.33x), beauty-workforce intensity (1.90x) and urban concentration (1.59x); a 1.82x local residual remains and is kept as evidence that the tricity is a makeup-academy hot-spot. Three harmonised methods (top-down ₹2.18 cr; CA-05 restated to makeup-led ₹3.96 cr; supply count ₹3.67 cr) give the adopted band of **₹1.45 / 3.27 / 5.28 cr**.

**Rule for every slide and file:** the tricity makeup-training market is ₹3.27 cr base (≈730 learners). CA-05's ₹5.7 cr stays as the wider "makeup + beauty-artistry" upper reference and is never called the makeup market. The chat-stage desk estimate for India (₹800–1,500 cr) is superseded and must not be reused.

## 7. What the numbers mean for the strategy

1. **The tricity is enough to start and not enough to finish.** At a 15–25% share, tricity tuition supports roughly ₹0.5–0.8 cr for one centre. PNA's plan reaches 13% of all tricity makeup-led learners by Year 3 and a 26% revenue share, because its realised fee is about 1.8x the tricity average; against the professional-programme tier it actually competes in, the share is about 20% [EST, reviewer], at the top of the 10–30% single-centre band. **H1 binds at the plan's fee by Year 3 unless the out-of-town share is delivered.** For the first 12–18 months, capacity, visibility, founder hours and execution bind first.
2. **The regional layers are where growth lives, and they are the least evidenced.** SAM-2 to SAM-4 add ₹4.5 cr at base to the ₹3.3 cr tricity, on reach and participation shares that are hypotheses. They are therefore tested with CRM city tags, two pop-up pilots and one Destination pilot cohort before any regional spend (gate G2).
3. **Delhi NCR is not in the SAM.** It has 7.4x the tricity's women aged 17–35 and 2.43x India's income, but rent is 3.5x, premium competitors publish ₹2.25–5.95L prices, and bridal prices (₹25k median) are no higher than Chandigarh's, so a graduate earns no more there. It is a Stage-3 online/hybrid market at most.
4. **The online layer is real but not for now.** ₹7.9 cr of North-reachable online demand sits behind tiny tickets (₹499–2,999) and a cold national audience; PNA has no verified Instagram or YouTube presence. It becomes relevant when the owned audience exceeds ~50k engaged followers (gate condition).
5. **SOM is a plan, not a prophecy.** ₹1.88 cr incl. GST in Year 3 assumes the enrolment target is hit, both price steps land, the regional engine passes G2 and capacity is added after G1. The planning case (G1 at M9) reaches ₹162.9L ex-GST in Year 3 with all gates passed; ₹54.1L of that (33%) sits behind price step 2 and the gated layers, and the base business without them is ₹108.8L at roughly zero EBITDA. The Conservative outcome is ₹0.41 cr. The gates in `strategy/recommended_strategy.md` §7 decide which path PNA is on by month 9.
