# International Benchmarks: Scalable Models in Beauty/Makeup Education and Adjacent Services

**Prepared by:** International Benchmark Agent · **Date:** 2026-10-16 · **For:** Priyanka Nanda Makeup Academy (PNA), Chandigarh · **Feeds:** Gate A (Evidence); hypotheses H3, H4, H5 in `project_plan.md`
**Source register:** `research/source_fragments/sources_international.md` (IDs S-INT-xx; every source accessed 2026-10-16).

**Tags:** **[FACT]** verified in a primary filing/registry/official page or reputable reporting · **[CLAIM]** company-authored, commissioned, secondary-only or seen only in a search summary · **[EST]** our arithmetic from cited inputs (shown) · **[HYP]** hypothesis to test.
**Conventions:** ₹12 per RMB is an illustrative rate for translating fees **[EST, verify before use]**; US$1 = ₹85 as used in the Urban Company RHP (S-INT-11). Short cites like (S-INT-01) point to the register.

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Mao Geping (China) is the best-evidenced founder-academy case and its lesson is about role, not scale.** Founded October 2000, the institute network is still only nine owned campuses (a tenth in preparation) after 24 years. In 2024 it enrolled more than 6,000 students and booked RMB151.7M of training and related sales, which is 3.9% of group revenue, at a 69.0% gross margin **[FACT]** (S-INT-01, S-INT-02). The listed company's revenue is overwhelmingly product (RMB3.73B in 2024). The school is the credential, talent and brand-trust engine for a product business, not the profit engine.
2. **Premium-academy economics are capacity-bound.** Revenue per student is about RMB21–25k, roughly ₹2.5–3.0 lakh **[EST]**. In H1 2025 training revenue fell 5.9% because management capped enrolment and class occupancy and stopped charging a pre-exam fee, and gross margin fell to 62.9% because of higher staff costs from a larger faculty **[FACT]** (S-INT-02). Even this famous brand holds about 1% of China's RMB9.8B makeup-training market **[EST]**.
3. **US chains and franchises (Paul Mitchell, Aveda, Empire) are not a template for India.** They sit on state licensure-hour rules, federal student aid and product-distribution agendas. WBEZ reports that Illinois for-profit cosmetology schools hold the training route to required licensing exams and that median 10-year earnings sit below the high-school median **[FACT]** (S-INT-40). A Paul Mitchell franchise is reported at US$1.0–1.4M investment **[CLAIM]**. Aveda sold its last two owned institutes in 2019 and kept the brand, curriculum and distribution **[FACT]** (S-INT-32). The portable kernel is "brand + curriculum + trainer certification", not capex-heavy campuses.
4. **Pure digital courses rarely pay in beauty; creators monetise through products.** Lisa Eldridge and Huda Kattan built audiences with free tutorials and then launched product brands (2018 and 2013) (S-INT-45, S-INT-48). MasterClass and Skillshare needed US$100M+ of venture funding each, and both reversed course on pricing or headcount (S-INT-46, S-INT-47). QC Makeup Academy claims 8,000+ students and grads but names no accrediting body (S-INT-42). No completion-rate evidence for makeup courses was found, so **H4 (hybrid beats online on completion and price realisation) is supported in direction by the Mao Geping funnel design but not proven [HYP]**.
5. **Hybrid + third-party certification is the scalable institutional layer.** VTCT reports 3,000+ training locations and 120,000+ learners a year (S-INT-43). India's Beauty & Wellness Sector Skill Council reports 2.55M enrolled and 2.07M certified (S-INT-44). Urban Company certifies its in-house trained professionals through NSDC (S-INT-11). For PNA, affiliating to a recognised credential is cheaper and faster than building one.
6. **Marketplace verdict: structurally hard, not a next step (supports H5).** Urban Company, the one large survivor in this sample, took about ten years from incorporation (Dec 2014) to first positive adjusted EBITDA (FY25). It spends 22.75% of its services revenue on professional onboarding and training, earns services revenue of roughly 24–27% of transaction value **[EST]**, and runs core India services at 3.3% (FY25) to 6.9% (Q1 FY27) adjusted EBITDA margin on transaction value **[FACT]** (S-INT-12, S-INT-13). Smaller peers failed or shrank: Blow LTD was liquidated (2024) and dissolved (Jan 2026), Vaniday shut its non-Singapore markets in 2019, Glamsquad survived only after a pandemic restructuring, and Priv merged to gain scale.
7. **Supplier economics are thin for the people PNA would graduate.** Urban Company reports average partner net earnings of ₹28,322 a month over 91 hours (about ₹313/hour) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-15). A company-commissioned Redseer study puts offline salon beauticians at ₹12,000–21,000 a month **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-11). A 2024 investigation reported about ₹45,000 for a beautician's training and product costs plus ratings-linked ID blocking **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-16). A marketplace is an outlet for alumni, not a fix for their earnings.
8. **The founder is both the conversion asset and the scaling constraint (supports H3).** Mao's spouse and co-founder ran the institute day to day (principal 2000–2010) **[FACT]** (S-INT-01), a student complained the founder appeared only for graduation photos **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-05), and the flagship brand is 99.3% of revenue **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-07). Glauca Rossi, a London academy founded in 1989 with Charlotte Tilbury among its alumni, now states it is "no longer offering courses" with no reason given **[FACT]** (S-INT-41).
9. **Conduct and trust risk is real in price-sensitive markets.** Chinese media reported Mao Geping students facing fee pressure and bundled product purchases; two school entities were de-registered for undisclosed reasons **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03, S-INT-04). US students reported misleading recruiters and hidden fees (S-INT-40). Optional kits, published fees and honest outcome claims are low-cost protections.
10. **Most transferable to PNA, in order:** (1) academy-as-funnel with adjacent revenue (bridal/pro services, kits, pro-membership); (2) low-price taster to offline flagship conversion; (3) third-party credential affiliation (B&WSSC/NSDC, international awarding bodies); (4) alumni/pro-card partnerships with brands; (5) licensed satellites only after the curriculum is codified (a later, lower-confidence step). Not transferable now: marketplace, US-style franchise, platform-scale digital courses.

## 3. Case notes

### A. Offline academies and founder-brand schools

#### A1. Mao Geping (毛戈平) Institutes of Makeup Artistry, China
- **Model/geography.** Owned institutes in Hangzhou, Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, Chongqing, Wuhan, Zhengzhou, Shenzhen and Qingdao; a tenth (Guangzhou) was in preparation in 2025 **[FACT]** (S-INT-02, S-INT-03). The institute was set up in October 2000 by Mao Geping, three months after the brand's predecessor company; his wife Wang Liqun, a co-founder and director, was executive principal 2000–2010 and has been general manager of Mao Geping Image Design since 2010 **[FACT]** (S-INT-01). The listed group sells cosmetics through 378 self-operated and 31 distributor counters staffed by 2,800+ beauty advisors **[FACT]** (S-INT-01).
- **How it scaled.** Owned, not franchised, and slowly: nine campuses in 24 years **[EST]**. The group's growth came from products. Training was reported at 9.5–13.6% of main-business revenue in 2014–2017 H1 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-04) versus 3.9% of group revenue in 2024 **[FACT]** (S-INT-01); the definitions differ, so read this as direction only. The company says training "bolsters our brand image and reputation" and supplies "new talents" to product development, and it earmarked HK$151.43M (6.0% of IPO net proceeds) for more institutes, almost all unspent at end-2024 **[FACT]** (S-INT-01).
- **Pricing.** 2022 prospectus fees: 5-month course RMB17,800; 1–1.5 year image-design courses RMB33,800–34,800; shorter foundation courses mostly under RMB6,000 **[CLAIM, via S-INT-03]**. A student blogger reported 2023 prices of RMB15,800 (3 months), RMB19,800 (5 months) and RMB32,800 (1 year) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03). Beijing's 1.5-year specialty course was RMB33,800 in 2025 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-05). Optional kits cost RMB3,609–3,894 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03). Online entry courses were sold at ¥1 group-buy (list ¥299) and ¥9.9 flash sale (list ¥398), mainly to drive offline enrolment **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03).
- **Volumes.** Students: 4,008 (2020), 3,667 (2021), 2,493 (2022), about 4,980 (2023, derived from +20.4%), more than 6,000 (2024), more than 3,800 in H1 2025 (+0.77%) **[FACT/EST]** (S-INT-03, S-INT-01, S-INT-02).
- **Economics.** Training and related sales: RMB104.1M (2023, 3.6% of revenue) and RMB151.7M (2024, 3.9%); gross margin 64.0% then 69.0% **[FACT]** (S-INT-01). H1 2025: RMB67.3M (-5.9%), gross margin 62.9% versus 72.4% a year earlier, with revenue attributed to enrolment and occupancy control plus the end of a pre-exam training fee, and margin to higher staff costs from a larger faculty **[FACT]** (S-INT-02). Derived: revenue per student about RMB25k at most in 2024 (151.7M / 6,000; "related sales" are included, so this is an upper bound) and about RMB21k in 2023; about 670 students and RMB16.9M of revenue per institute; about 27 students and RMB0.67M revenue per training staff member a year **[EST]**. At ₹12/RMB, total training revenue is about ₹182 crore and per student about ₹3.0 lakh **[EST]**. Historic tuition and product pull-through: cosmetics sold through the training channel were about 51–57% of tuition revenue in 2015–2017 H1 (22.3M / 39.1M; 21.5M / 42.1M) **[EST from S-INT-04]**. Frost & Sullivan (cited in the annual report) sizes China's makeup-training market at RMB9.8B (2023) growing to RMB19.3B by 2028 **[CLAIM]**; Mao Geping's RMB104.1M is about 1.1% of the 2023 figure **[EST]**.
- **Quality control.** Training staff of 226; faculty expanded and occupancy capped in 2025; in-person delivery with online as a supplement **[FACT]** (S-INT-02). Diplomas carry the founder's electronic signature **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-06). Teaching uses the brand's own products, and students said they had to buy specified palettes **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-05).
- **Funnel to the brand.** The first counters were staffed by school-trained advisors, and many advisors come from the school **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03, S-INT-08). Staff-led makeup demonstrations anchor a membership model in which about 36,000 top members drove 32% of 2024 offline revenue **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-05).
- **What worked.** A founder with national credibility (TV makeup work from 1995) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-07); a spouse running operations; school as credential and talent pipeline; a social "before/after" transformation that students post online; and a low-price online entry that feeds the paid offline course.
- **What went wrong / risks.** Since 2021, media reported "PUA" tactics on students, aggressive fees and bundling of poor-quality cosmetics; the company did not respond; two school entities (Sichuan, Zhejiang) were abnormally de-registered, with reasons undisclosed **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-04). Enrolment growth stalled in 2025. Flagship-brand dependence is 99.3% of revenue and the founder-name dependence is acute **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-07, S-INT-06). One student complained the founder rarely appears **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-05). A secondary summary puts FY2025 training revenue near RMB147M (-2.8%); not verified **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-10).
- **Transferability: Medium (pattern), Low (scale).** *Price sensitivity:* fees are premium and rest on a national celebrity brand; PNA must test willingness-to-pay locally. *Trust:* Mao's trust came from TV credits; PNA's is local reputation and visible student outcomes. *Practical skills:* the in-person lab is the product, so scale is capacity-limited. *Regulation:* China's private-training rules appear to have affected school entities; India's regime is lighter **[HYP, not verified]**. *Language:* no barrier; Hindi/Punjabi content helps. **Portable ideas:** school as funnel to services/products; cheap online entry; capacity control as a quality signal; founder plus operator pairing. **Avoid:** compulsory product purchase and "guaranteed learning" claims.

#### A2. Chinese peer chains (Dongtian, Baimeihui, Jimmy)
- **Facts.** A 2023 report counted Dongtian (3 schools), Baimeihui (3) and Jimmy (1) against Mao Geping's 9 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-03). No fees, volumes or economics were retrieved. **Takeaway [HYP]:** even leading private makeup-school brands stay at single-digit campuses; multi-city scale is the exception. **Transferability: Low (thin evidence).**

#### A3. Glauca Rossi, London (founded 1989)
- **Facts.** The official site states the school "is no longer offering courses", credits it with shaping many professionals including Charlotte Tilbury, and says the site will become a legacy space. No closure date or reason is given **[FACT]** (S-INT-41).
- **Takeaway [HYP]:** a founder-named academy is exposed to key-person and succession risk even with famous alumni; the cause of closure is unknown and should not be assumed. **Transferability: Low** as a model; high value as a warning for H3.

#### A4. Empire Beauty Schools, US
- **Model.** Family-owned, multi-campus chain; the company marked its 100th school in September 2010 (20 states), with about 100,000 people educated since founding and 20,000+ that year **[FACT, dated]** (S-INT-37, S-INT-38). Current programmes include a hybrid cosmetology route (S-INT-38). Campus-level tuition for 1,500 hours was reported at about US$17,000–19,260 (2024-25) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-39).
- **Context.** Demand is created by state licensure hours and funded by federal student aid. WBEZ found that Illinois for-profit cosmetology-type programmes cost about US$14,700 a year, and median earnings 10 years after enrolment ranged US$15,420–34,368 versus a high-school-graduate median of US$34,591; more than 80% of Illinois programmes are for-profit; as many as 32 programmes would have failed the federal Gainful Employment test using 2019 data **[FACT]** (S-INT-40).
- **Transferability: Low.** India has no equivalent licensure-hours captive demand or aid system **[HYP, not verified]**; the cautionary lesson is that fee levels must be justified by graduate earnings.

### B. Franchise and brand-owner networks

#### B1. Paul Mitchell Schools, US
- **Facts.** The official franchise page (search summary only) says each school is independently owned and operated, with "over 90" schools across 36 states **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-35). An aggregator lists a US$75,000 franchise fee, US$1,020,200–1,433,000 total investment from FDD Item 7, US$225,000 minimum cash and two weeks of HQ training; the page carries placeholder fields (zero units), so treat it as indicative only **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-36). Royalty and marketing-fee rates were not obtained. Sources disagree on the start date (2000 vs 2001).
- **Transferability: Low.** Capex per campus is far beyond a Chandigarh academy, and the model rides on licensure and product-brand pull. **Portable kernel [HYP]:** franchisor-provided curriculum, trainer certification and marketing, with a fee that scales with sales.

#### B2. Aveda Institutes, US
- **Facts.** Aveda is part of Estée Lauder (acquired 1997 for US$300M, per Wikipedia) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-34). In January 2019 there were 64 Aveda-branded schools, of which distributor-linked Neill Corp. owned 15; Aveda agreed to sell its last two owned Institutes (Minneapolis, SoHo) to Neill's Beauty Basics **[FACT]** (S-INT-32). Aveda's site claims 63 schools and 7,000+ graduates a year **[CLAIM, snippet only]** (S-INT-33).
- **Takeaway.** The brand owner moved from running schools to licensing the brand and standards to operators who also sell its products **[FACT/HYP]**. **Transferability: Medium** for the "brand-partner + operator" logic; **Low** for campuses.

### C. Hybrid and credential models

#### C1. VTCT Skills (UK)
- **Facts.** Educational charity (registered 1986) and awarding organisation with 3,000+ training locations, 120,000+ learners a year, 17 sectors; first UK beauty training school dated to 1962; acquired Skillsfirst in 2022; the VTCT Skills brand launched November 2024 **[FACT]** (S-INT-43). Ofqual status and makeup-specific qualifications were not confirmed.
- **Model [HYP].** Approved centres deliver; the awarding body sets standards and assesses. **Transferability: Medium-High** as an affiliation route, subject to checking whether UK/International credentials carry weight with Indian employers.

#### C2. Beauty & Wellness Sector Skill Council (B&WSSC) and NSDC (India)
- **Facts.** B&WSSC reports 2,552,358+ candidates enrolled, 2,175,422+ assessed and 2,070,921+ certified; "Makeup" appears as a Beauty & Salon sub-sector (S-INT-44) **[CLAIM, undated counters]**. Urban Company trains and certifies through an NSDC agreement dated 8 Dec 2022 **[FACT]** (S-INT-11).
- **Transferability: Medium-High.** A recognised Indian credential is inexpensive and aligns with employer expectations; fee levels in scheme-linked training are typically low **[HYP, not verified]**, so PNA should treat certification as a trust badge, not a revenue line.

#### C3. QC Makeup Academy (Canada/online)
- **Facts.** Home page claims 8,000+ students and grads, a "Start for $49" entry, "internationally recognized" certifications with no accreditor named, graduates working at MAC, Benefit and Sephora, and discounts from "preferred makeup partners" **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-42). No in-person component is described; completion rates, refunds and fees were not shown.
- **Transferability: Medium** for theory and discovery content; **Low** as a stand-alone credential in India, where the buyer needs hands-on practice and an accepted certificate **[HYP]**.

### D. Creator and platform models

#### D1. Lisa Eldridge and Huda Kattan
- **Facts.** Eldridge: YouTube channel from 2010 (about 2.07M subscribers, Jan 2025), corporate creative-director roles (Boots No7, Lancôme), own brand launched 2018 with three lipsticks, MBE 2025 (S-INT-45). Kattan: blog April 2010, cosmetics line 2013 launched via Sephora (S-INT-48). Neither source reports revenue or course income **[CLAIM, Wikipedia]**.
- **Takeaway [HYP]:** free education builds the audience and the paid asset is the product brand. **Transferability: Medium** for content-led lead generation; **Low** for building a product brand without capital or distribution.

#### D2. MasterClass and Skillshare
- **Facts.** MasterClass (founded 2014): about US$240M raised by May 2020, US$225M Series F in May 2021 at a reported US$2.75B valuation, about 20% staff cut in June 2022; Bobbi Brown is listed as an instructor (S-INT-46). Skillshare (2010): US$108M raised by 2020, moved to US$9.95/month in 2014, ended free classes in Sept 2021, new CEO Aug 2025 (S-INT-47) **[CLAIM, Wikipedia]**.
- **Takeaway:** platform learning is capital-intensive and celebrity-driven; beauty is a minor category. **Transferability: Low**; potentially a distribution channel for a short course **[HYP]**.

### E. Marketplaces and on-demand services (see section 5 for economics)

#### E1. Urban Company, India
- **Model.** Full-stack services marketplace: it vets, trains, equips and rates independent professionals, and charges them a commission (marketplace categories are recognised on a take-rate basis, unlike principal-basis categories) **[FACT]** (S-INT-14). It began in 2014 as UrbanClap with at-home beauty (S-INT-18). Listed on NSE/BSE on 17 September 2025 (S-INT-18).
- **Scale.** 51 cities across India, UAE and Singapore at 30 June 2025; 47 in India; 54,347 average monthly active professionals in Q1 FY26 (S-INT-12). Q1 FY27 NTV ₹1,465 Cr, revenue ₹528 Cr, 13.2M orders (S-INT-13).
- **Training and QC.** 339 permanent trainers across 17 super-categories; 247 classrooms in 17 cities; about 1.23M training hours in Q1 FY26; new-professional training followed by mentor shadowing; re-training for low ratings; NSDC-certified; background checks **[FACT]** (S-INT-11).
- **Pricing/fees.** A 2024 investigation reported ₹45,000 for beautician training (fee plus practice products), ₹6,000–15,000 for in-person re-training, ratings floors of 4.7, 70% lead-acceptance rules and compulsory product purchases **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-16). A Competition Commission of India order of 24 March 2021 closed a complaint about product-purchase conditions without finding abuse (dominance was not established) **[FACT]** (S-INT-11).
- **Beauty specifically.** Management describes beauty as a steady, "more mature" growth category (S-INT-14). The India beauty and wellness services market is ₹575–600B (about US$7B) in FY2025, growing 9–10% a year **[CLAIM, Redseer for UC]** (S-INT-11).
- **Transferability: Low to build; Medium as a placement channel.** *Capital and time:* about ten years to breakeven. *Trust:* works because UC controls quality and brand. *Practical skills:* UC trains its own recruits, so an academy's graduates still go through UC's training **[HYP]**. *Regulation:* labour notices in Bengaluru and Mumbai are disclosed in the prospectus **[FACT]** (S-INT-12).

#### E2. Glamsquad (US)
- **Facts.** Launched 2014; raised about US$24M (2018) to US$28M (2023) per Crunchbase quoted by the press; acquired Veluxe in 2018; claimed 1,700+ freelance stylists and 700,000+ appointments in 2019 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-19, S-INT-20, S-INT-22). In January 2023 it operated in six cities after "stabilizing the Company and restructuring the business" during the pandemic, and claimed 18 months of profitability **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-21, S-INT-22). A projected US$20M 2023 revenue came from "industry sources" and is weak **[CLAIM]**.
- **Correction to the brief:** sources show Glamsquad did **not** shut down; it contracted and restructured.
- **Transferability: Low.**

#### E3. Priv / BeGlammed (US)
- **Facts.** June 2019 merger into a single app across 32 markets; claimed 500,000 customers and 30,000+ beauty professionals; revenues "pretty equal"; a mandatory 20% gratuity; manicures from US$40, bridal from US$125; financial terms undisclosed **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-19). A September 2019 report quotes Priv claiming profit and flags California AB5 gig-labour exposure **[CLAIM, snippet only]** (S-INT-31). Later status was not verified.
- **Transferability: Low.** Merging to consolidate suggests sub-scale economics in standalone markets **[HYP]**.

#### E4. Vaniday (Germany/Singapore)
- **Facts.** Started in Brazil in March 2015 and Singapore in December 2015; never profitable since its first investment; transactions and revenue grew 2.5x in 2018; in June 2019 it retained only Singapore after closing Australia, Italy, UAE, Russia and Brazil **[FACT/CLAIM]** (S-INT-23). Later coverage reports a December 2019 Singapore closure and a July 2020 relaunch by a new owner **[CLAIM, snippet only]** (S-INT-24).
- **Transferability: Low.**

#### E5. Blow LTD and Ruuby (UK)
- **Blow LTD** (incorporated 15 April 2013): at-home blow-dry and beauty services, "cut and blow dry" from GBP69 in 2019 (S-INT-26); reported funding of GBP3.5M from Unilever Ventures (2017) and a GBP1.93M crowdfund from 497 investors (Sept 2019) **[CLAIM, snippet only]** (S-INT-27). Companies House shows last accounts to 30 April 2021, a resolution to wind up on 8 October 2024 (creditors' voluntary liquidation), a final meeting in October 2025 and dissolution on 7 January 2026 **[FACT]** (S-INT-25).
- **Ruuby Limited** (incorporated 25 February 2014): still active, filing small-company accounts to December 2025 and making small share allotments in 2025–26 **[FACT]** (S-INT-28). Scale and profitability are unknown.
- **Transferability: Low.** A decade-long record in the UK shows one liquidated, one surviving at small scale.

#### E6. Treatwell and Fresha (UK/EU; global)
- **Treatwell terms (September 2026).** Commission applies to *New Bookings*; repeat bookings are commission-free; a customer counts as new if not in the partner's database or without a successful appointment in the last 365 days; commission can apply for 90 days if Treatwell suspects steering to direct booking; the percentage sits in a private cooperation agreement **[FACT]** (S-INT-29). Third-party sources quoting 35% are not verified.
- **Fresha.** 20% one-time fee on brand-new marketplace clients (US$6 minimum); returning clients are free; subscriptions US$19.95 (independent) or US$14.95 per bookable team member; card processing 2.29–2.79% + US$0.20 **[FACT]** (S-INT-30).
- **Takeaway [HYP]:** booking marketplaces monetise the *first* booking and the software, because the client-stylist relationship is portable. **Transferability: Medium** as tools PNA or its alumni can use; **Low** to build.

### F. Brand-partner and alumni programmes

## 5. Marketplace model: evidence on difficulty and economics

### 5.1 Urban Company, the only scaled survivor (all figures from filings unless stated)
| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | Q1 FY26 (Jun-25) | Q1 FY27 (Jun-26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| India consumer services NTV (₹ Cr) | 1,805 | 2,216 | 2,667 | 817 | 1,056 (ex-InstaHelp) |
| Services revenue ÷ NTV **[EST]** | 24.1% | 25.4% | 26.1% | 27.2% | n/a |
| Contribution margin (% NTV) | 17.7% | 19.6% | 20.2% | 20.3% | n/a |
| Segment adjusted EBITDA (% NTV) | -9.7% | -0.5% | 3.3% | 4.0% | 6.9% |
| Onboarding cost (% of India services revenue) | 34.2% | 24.4% | 22.8% | 23.1% | n/a |
| Avg monthly active professionals (India services) | 41,177 | 44,464 | 45,619 | 51,875 | n/a |
Sources: S-INT-12 (abridged prospectus tables), S-INT-11 (onboarding costs), S-INT-13 (Q1 FY27). Ratios marked **[EST]** are our arithmetic: services revenue ÷ NTV, e.g. FY25 6,948.22 / 26,671.95 = 26.1%.

Key points:
- **No single "take rate" is published.** Blended revenue ÷ NTV is 35.0% consolidated in FY25 (11,444.65 / 32,709.14) **[EST]**, but this mixes marketplace commission with principal-basis categories (pest control, wall panels), product sales to professionals, subscriptions and Native hardware; management says marketplace categories are recognised on a take-rate basis and "take rates remained broadly stable" (S-INT-14). Marketplace services revenue is therefore roughly **24–27% of NTV** **[EST]**. Inc42 reports commissions and fees of 28.3% of partners' average gross earnings in 9M FY26 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-17); Al Jazeera reported "up to 25%" in 2024 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-16). Treat 25–28% as the plausible commission band **[EST]**.
- **Time and capital.** Incorporated 22 Dec 2014 (S-INT-12); consolidated adjusted EBITDA was -₹298 Cr (FY23), -₹119 Cr (FY24) and +₹12 Cr (FY25) **[FACT]**. FY25 profit after tax of ₹240 Cr included a ₹211 Cr deferred tax credit, against profit before tax of ₹28.6 Cr (S-INT-12). Individual funding rounds of US$50M–255M are listed for 2018–2021 **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-18).
- **Supply side is a cost centre.** Onboarding costs (classrooms, trainers, materials, referral and search costs) were ₹158 Cr in FY25, equal to 22.75% of India services revenue and about 5.9% of NTV **[FACT/EST]**; incentives to professionals were a further 6.6% of consolidated revenue (S-INT-11).
- **New lines can swamp the core.** In Q1 FY27 the consolidated business excluding InstaHelp earned adjusted EBITDA of ₹67 Cr, but InstaHelp's ₹(132) Cr loss left consolidated adjusted EBITDA at ₹(65) Cr; InstaHelp lost ₹346 per order, with an average order value of ₹138 against a management breakeven near ₹300 **[FACT/CLAIM]** (S-INT-13, S-INT-17).
- **Beauty is a mature category for the leader.** Management calls beauty a steady, "more mature" growth profile (S-INT-14). Management's long-term target for India consumer services is 9–10% adjusted EBITDA on NTV, against 6.9% in Q1 FY27 (S-INT-14, S-INT-13).
- **Provider economics.** Average net in-hand ₹28,322 a month at 91 hours (up from 59 hours in FY22) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-15, S-INT-14). NTV per active professional-month in FY25 was about ₹48.7k (26,671.95M / (45,619 × 12)) **[EST]**. Before product, travel and commission deductions, a professional's earning gap versus an offline salon rests on utilisation: Redseer, commissioned by UC, says online beauticians earn ₹22–30k versus ₹12–21k offline **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-11).
- **Retention and leakage.** The share of NTV from repeat users rose from 72.1% (FY22) to 84.2% (9M FY26) **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-14), and the RHP lists customer and professional circumvention of platform fees as a risk **[FACT]** (S-INT-11).
- **Labour and conduct exposure.** The prospectus discloses Labour Commissioner notices in Bengaluru and Mumbai in 2025, including a complaint from service professionals about their engagement terms **[FACT]** (S-INT-12). Investigative reporting describes ID blocking, ratings floors and product-purchase rules **[CLAIM]** (S-INT-16).

### 5.3 Verdict **[EST/HYP]**
A beauty-services marketplace is a different company from an academy: two-sided, density-dependent, supply-training heavy, labour-regulated and slow to profit. In this sample, the one scaled winner needed a decade, hundreds of millions of dollars of private capital and an unusual density of demand; smaller or regional peers shrank, merged or were liquidated. **H5 is strongly supported.** For PNA the relevant options are (a) place alumni on existing platforms, (b) use booking software, (c) run its own bridal/pro-services desk as a *service line of the academy* rather than a two-sided platform.

### 6.2 Implications for PNA (all **[HYP]** until tested in the business-model workstream)
1. **Design the academy as the core of a small ecosystem.** Mao Geping's pattern (credential → talent → services/products) fits a founder-led academy. Candidates to cost: a bridal/pro-services desk staffed by top alumni, an optional kit and brand-partner programme, and an alumni membership.
2. **Build a price ladder.** Publish a low-price workshop or recorded taster that converts to the flagship offline course; evidence is the Mao Geping funnel (¥1/¥9.9 offers versus RMB17,800+ courses) and QC's $49 entry. Conversion data from PNA's own funnel is needed.
3. **Treat capacity as a quality lever.** Mao Geping's cap on occupancy raised service quality at the cost of revenue growth; its ratios (about 27 students per training staff member a year; about 670 students per institute) give a reference for PNA's batch sizes and trainer ratios **[EST]**.
4. **Reduce founder dependency deliberately.** Codify the curriculum, certify senior trainers, schedule founder-led master days and be honest about who teaches; the Mao Geping student complaint shows the cost of over-promising founder presence.
5. **Affiliate for credentials rather than build one.** Check B&WSSC/NSDC affiliation terms and whether an international awarding body (VTCT/ITEC) is recognised by Indian employers and clients; confirm the Indian regulatory position for makeup training.
6. **Protect trust.** Do not require product purchases; publish fees and kit costs; avoid guarantees; publish placement and income outcomes. Chinese and US student complaints cluster on bundling, hidden fees and misleading promises.
7. **Do not build a marketplace now.** Use Urban Company and salon chains as placement outlets, and learn their onboarding standards. Evidence that UC retrains every recruit means an academy certificate may shorten but not skip onboarding **[HYP]**.
8. **Expansion path.** If Chandigarh capacity binds, test a single pilot with a codified curriculum and quality audits before any licensing; Mao Geping's pace (nine campuses in 24 years) and Aveda's exit from owned campuses both argue for staged, partner-funded expansion.
9. **Data PNA should supply to complete the benchmarking:** revenue and gross margin per batch, fee levels by course, completion and placement rates, alumni incomes, share of revenue from services, and enquiry-to-enrolment conversion.
