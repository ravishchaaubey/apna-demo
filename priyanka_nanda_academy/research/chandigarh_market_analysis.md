## 1. Executive summary

1. **[FACT] Tricity is a small slice of the northern market.** Chandigarh UT is projected at about 1.27 million in March 2026 (+20% on Census 2011). Adding all of SAS Nagar (Mohali) and Panchkula districts gives about 3.2 million [EST] (S-CHD-01, 03, 04; A-CHD-01). Women aged 17-35 in that catchment number about **0.47 million [EST]**. That is roughly **2.9% of the 16.1 million women aged 17-35 in Punjab, Haryana, HP, J&K, Chandigarh and Delhi** [EST].
2. **[FACT] Affluence is high in the city and mixed around it.** Per-capita NSDP 2023-24 (current prices): Chandigarh ₹4.30 lakh (2.28x India), Delhi ₹4.59 lakh (2.43x), Haryana ₹3.19 lakh (1.69x), HP ₹2.35 lakh (1.24x), **Punjab ₹1.95 lakh (1.03x, about the India average)**, J&K ₹1.40 lakh (0.74x) (S-CHD-06). Punjab is not a rich feeder market on this measure.
3. **[FACT] Chandigarh is an education destination, but beauty-specific origin data does not exist.** Chandigarh's higher-education GER is 64.8 (female 75.4) against 28.4 (female 28.5) for India (S-CHD-11). A Tribune report said "nearly one lakh students from across the region" study in Chandigarh, "particularly from Punjab, Haryana and Himachal Pradesh" (S-CHD-17, 2010, old). [HYP] This supports destination learning. No source gives the origin of makeup or beauty students.
4. **[FACT] Bridal makeup price levels are similar across North India.** Median "per function" price among the top-ranked ~20 WedMeGood listings: Chandigarh ₹25,000, Mohali ₹20,000, Ludhiana ₹21,000, Amritsar ₹25,000, Delhi-NCR ₹25,000 (S-CHD-12, 13). Chandigarh has no price premium over Punjab cities or Delhi on this sample.
5. **[CLAIM] Local supply is dense.** WedMeGood claims 600+ bridal makeup professionals for Chandigarh (about 127 per 100k women aged 17-35, against about 87 for Delhi on the same platform's "3,000+" claim). UrbanPro lists 111 makeup-class listings in Chandigarh (mostly individual tutors) and 1,438+ in Delhi (S-CHD-12, 15). Counts are platform-specific and not comparable to institute counts.
6. **[EST] Demand estimate.** Tricity paid makeup/beauty-artistry training is about **1,250 students and ₹5.7 crore a year in the base case** (low about 600 students and ₹1.8 crore; high about 2,200 students and ₹13.4 crore). **The prior desk estimate (₹4-10 crore, 500-1,500 students) is confirmed for the base case. Its range is widened on both sides.** Tricity equals about 0.5% of India by our population-and-affluence method (range 0.35-0.75%), consistent with the prior 0.4-0.8% share.
7. **[EST] A single centre has a hard ceiling in tricity.** A 10% share of the base market is about 125 students and ₹0.57 crore. A 30% share is about 375 students and ₹1.7 crore. A ₹5 crore training-revenue ambition would need about 88% of the base market.
8. **[FACT] Rents are moderate outside the prime sectors.** Non-prime Chandigarh retail/showroom asking rents are about ₹47/sq ft/month (median, n=14), Mohali about ₹62, Zirakpur about ₹57. The one Sector 17 showroom listing is ₹192, and Sector 34 showrooms are ₹112-130. Delhi's median is about ₹167, Gurgaon ₹105 and Noida ₹94 (S-CHD-22; asking rents from one portal, low-to-medium reliability).
9. **[FACT/EST] Outstation students face real costs and safety concerns.** PG rooms run ₹8,000-17,500 a month (Aug 2026). Only 1 of 11 sampled listings was girls-only. A 2020 Tribune letters page described "thousands of PGs" with only 25 licensed, and a fatal PG fire (S-CHD-18, 20). A two-to-three-month residential course adds roughly ₹20-50k of living cost [EST].
10. **Verdict on H1: SUPPORTED for any training-revenue ambition above about ₹2 crore a year.** Tricity is a strong, affluent base and a plausible destination hub. It is too small to be the whole growth story for a single centre. The founder's ambition figure is still needed to give a hard yes or no.

**Chandigarh, Mohali and Panchkula population notes.**

- Chandigarh UT Census 2011 is 10,55,450 (+17.19% on 2001) [FACT] (S-CHD-02, 29). The report's own figure of 1,056k is consistent. Chandigarh's 2026 projection is 12.67 lakh; aggregators give 12.62-12.70 lakh (S-CHD-28, low reliability). The 2036 projection is 13.60 lakh.
- Mohali: SAS Nagar district 9,94,628 in Census 2011, sex ratio 878, literacy 84.9% [FACT] (S-CHD-03). Mohali city (urban agglomeration) is 1,76,152 and Zirakpur 95,553 (S-CHD-02/23). A provisional Tribune figure of 9,86,147 (April 2011) was later revised (S-CHD-05).
- Panchkula district: 5,61,293 on the district website, labelled provisional, sex ratio 873, 55.8% urban [FACT] (S-CHD-04). A Tribune item cites 5,58,890. We use about 5.6 lakh.
- Wikipedia's "tricity" figure (over 1.61 million, 2011) covers urban areas only [FACT] (S-CHD-02). Our catchment uses whole districts to include Kharar, Zirakpur, Dera Bassi, Pinjore and Kalka. District rural populations lower the effective uptake rate. A-CHD-03 is set on the blended population to absorb this.
- Growth rates for Mohali and Panchkula are our assumptions (A-CHD-01). The state rates are 0.81% for Punjab and 1.42% for Haryana, and Chandigarh's own rate is 1.22%. Satellites have grown faster than their states. The 2026 district sensitivity is 11.2-13.4 lakh for Mohali (0.8-2.0% a year) and 6.7-7.5 lakh for Panchkula (1.2-2.0%).
- NCR total population was 58.2 million in Census 2011 (S-CHD-26). NCR-wide women aged 17-35 were not computed; Delhi NCT only is shown.

**Reading the affluence and participation data.**

- [FACT] Chandigarh and Delhi are 2.3-2.4x India on per-capita NSDP. Haryana is 1.7x. Punjab, the main feeder state, is at the national average. J&K is below it.
- [FACT] Female worker participation is very low in Delhi (12.2%), Haryana (22.8%) and Punjab (28.0%), and high in HP (62.2%) and J&K (49.6%). [HYP] In the low-participation states, a makeup career may be attractive as home-based self-employment, but family permission and mobility may limit outstation enrolment. This is untested.
- [FACT] Chandigarh's female GER of 75.4 is far above every neighbour (Delhi 49.7, HP 49.7, Haryana 37.0, Punjab 30.1, J&K 27.2). AISHE computes GER as enrolment over resident population aged 18-23. A value this high is consistent with inflow of non-resident students [HYP]. Chandigarh's female enrolment was 55,712 against about 73,900 resident females aged 18-23 implied by that GER (our arithmetic).
- [FACT] AISHE female enrolment 2021-22: Chandigarh 55,712; Punjab 4,24,128; Haryana 5,62,386; HP 1,72,508; J&K 2,07,469; Delhi 5,60,037 (S-CHD-11, Table 10).

**Implications.**

- [FACT] A typical Chandigarh bridal booking of ₹20-25k is the same as in Ludhiana, Amritsar and Delhi-NCR. There is no tricity price premium.

**Seasonality [CLAIM/HYP].**

- 4.6 million weddings in about 44 days (CAIT, S-CHD-16) shows how concentrated demand is.
- Peak months are expected to be roughly November-February, with a secondary peak in spring. This is general knowledge and was not verified with muhurat data (the Drik Panchang page returned 404).
- [HYP] Makeup-training enrolments likely peak before the season (summer and early monsoon) and fall during it, because trainees and instructors are busy with bookings. Founder admission data is needed to test this.

## 8. Draft catchment demand estimate

### 8.1 Tricity: bottom-up (paid professional makeup and beauty-artistry training, private market)

Formula: students = (women aged 17-35 in catchment x annual participation rate p) / (1 - outstation share s). Revenue = students x average realised fee.

| Parameter | Low | Base | High | ID |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Women 17-35, tricity-district catchment, 2026 | 420k | 471k | 520k | A-CHD-01, 02 |
| Annual participation in paid makeup/beauty-artistry course (resident women) | 0.12% | 0.20% | 0.30% | A-CHD-03 |
| Outstation share of tricity students | 15% | 25% | 30% | A-CHD-04 |
| Average realised fee per student | ₹30,000 | ₹45,000 | ₹60,000 | A-CHD-05 |

| Result | Low | Base | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resident students | 420,000 x 0.12% = 504 | 471,000 x 0.20% = 942 | 520,000 x 0.30% = 1,560 |
| Total students | 504 / 0.85 = **593** | 942 / 0.75 = **1,256** | 1,560 / 0.70 = **2,229** |
| of which outstation | 89 | 314 | 669 |
| Revenue (total x fee) | 593 x ₹30k = **₹1.8 crore** | 1,256 x ₹45k = **₹5.7 crore** | 2,229 x ₹60k = **₹13.4 crore** |

**Triangulations.**

1. *Share of India [EST].* Tricity holds 471k of India's 222.9M women aged 17-35 = 0.21%. The affluence weight is (185k x 2.28 + 183k x 1.5 + 103k x 1.69) / 471k = **1.85** (A-CHD-06; Mohali weight of 1.5 is our assumption). Resident share = 0.21% x 1.85 = **0.39%**. Adding 25% outstation inflow gives 0.39% / 0.75 = **0.52%**. This sits inside the prior 0.4-0.8%.
2. *Supply-side pipeline [EST/HYP].* 500-1,470 trainees a year (section 5; A-CHD-07). The base of 1,256 is inside this bracket.
3. *Fee reality check [FACT].* UrbanPro's Chandigarh page (9 Oct 2026) shows individual-tutor fees of ₹9,600-12,000 a month for 12 classes (group ₹7,680-9,600). A 2-3 month tutor course is therefore ₹15-36k, which supports a ₹30-45k average for short and mid-length courses. Academy fees were not verified here (other agents cover competitor pricing). Premium multi-month programmes would sit higher.

**Comparison with the prior desk estimate.**

| | Prior | Revised |
|---|---|---|
| Students a year | ~500-1,500 | **~600 / 1,250 / 2,200** (low/base/high) |
| Spend a year | ₹4-10 crore | **₹1.8 / 5.7 / 13.4 crore** |
| Share of India | 0.4-0.8% | **0.35-0.75%, base ~0.5%** |

**Verdict: base case confirmed; range widened.** The low and high ends are wider because participation rate (A-CHD-03) cannot be verified from any source found. It is the most important unknown. A single-day customer survey or admissions data from the founder and competitors would narrow it fastest.

### 8.2 What a single centre can capture (base market 1,256 students, ₹5.7 crore)

| Market share | Students a year | Training revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | 63 | ₹0.28 crore |
| 10% | 126 | ₹0.57 crore |
| 20% | 251 | ₹1.14 crore |
| 30% | 377 | ₹1.71 crore |

Share required for a target revenue: ₹1 crore needs 18% of the base market (8% of the high case). ₹2 crore needs 35% (16%). ₹3 crore needs 53% (25%). ₹5 crore needs 88% (41%). ₹10 crore is above the base market (82% of the high case).

### 8.3 Regional pool beyond tricity (offline reach and online/hybrid illustration)

| Pool (women 17-35, 2026) | 000 [EST] |
|---|---|
| Tricity catchment | 471 |
| Rest of Punjab, Haryana, HP and J&K (12,662 minus 286 counted in tricity) | 12,376 |
| Delhi NCT | 3,464 |

- [EST] The outstation draw implied by the base case is 314 students from a 12.4M pool, or about 1 in 39,000. That is a very low draw, so the destination hub is plausible but not a large source of volume at current reach.
- [HYP, illustration only] If a hybrid or online product reached the Punjab/Haryana/HP/J&K/Delhi pool at the same 0.20% participation, that is about 32,000 students a year, 25x the tricity base. The real conversion rate, price and completion for online or hybrid are unmeasured here and are covered by other agents. This number sizes headroom; it is not a forecast.

### 8.4 Limits of the estimate

- District populations include rural areas that probably participate less (A-CHD-01, 03).
- Women aged 17-35 is a convenience band. Actual buyers include women up to about 45 and some men.
- The estimate covers paid private training only. It excludes free government skilling (B&WSSC/PMKVY) and in-salon apprenticeships.

## 9. Verdict on H1 ("Chandigarh by itself is too small for the growth ambition")

**Verdict: SUPPORTED, with a condition. The founder's actual ambition figure is not in the project files, so the test is stated as a threshold.**

**Evidence for H1.**

- Tricity is 2.9% of the northern women-17-35 pool (A-CHD-01, section 8.3).
- Base training market is about ₹5.7 crore and about 1,250 students a year. One centre at 10-30% share yields ₹0.6-1.7 crore of fee revenue.
- A ₹2 crore target needs 35% of the whole base market and ₹5 crore needs 88%. These are not credible for one centre.
- Bridal MUA supply is already dense (about 127-200 platform listings per 100k women against about 87 in Delhi), and prices are no higher than elsewhere.
- Demand is concentrated into a short wedding season.
- Delhi NCT has 7.4x the pool, and three Punjab cities together have about 1.2x.

**Evidence against or softening H1.**

- Tricity is the most affluent and best-educated small market in the north (Chandigarh PCNSDP 2.28x India; female GER 75.4 against 28.5). A premium niche can win a high share.
- Chandigarh already works as an education destination for the region [HYP], which could lift outstation share above the 25% assumed.
- The outstation draw rate is tiny (1 in 39,000), so a modest rise in reach would add volume without a second centre.
- If the ambition is a premium single-centre business at ₹1-2 crore of fee revenue, tricity alone could be adequate at a 20-35% share. That needs strong brand dominance.

**Implications for the other options (to be tested by the economics and strategy agents) [HYP].**

- *Online or hybrid* is the only option that reaches the 16M-woman pool without a travel and PG cost. Outstation students currently add ₹20-48k of living cost to a ₹45k course, and few girls-only PG options exist.
- *A second centre in Ludhiana* has about half the tricity's pool at Punjab-average incomes. *A Delhi NCR centre* has 7x the pool at 2.4x income but is the most crowded and the highest-rent market (about 3.5x tricity retail rent).
- Both rely on the founder brand travelling. The tricity evidence cannot say whether it does.
