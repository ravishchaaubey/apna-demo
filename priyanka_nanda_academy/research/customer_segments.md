## 1. Executive summary

1. **Prioritise 3 core segments:** (P1) **aspiring professional MUAs aged 18–25**, including the **out-of-town (Punjab/HP/J&K/Haryana) overlay**. They bring the highest ticket and LTV. (P1b) **Working artists and salon staff who want to upskill.** They buy repeat masterclasses, cost little to acquire, and amplify the founder brand. (P2) **Career-switchers and homemakers.** Treat self-makeup learners and content creators as **seasonal, top-of-funnel and amplification** segments. **Defer govt-scheme vocational skilling.** It is a different, compliance-heavy B2G business. [REC]
2. **The ROI promise is credible but has to be evidenced, not asserted.** Established Tricity bridal MUAs list **₹14k–45k per function (median ₹25k)** on WedMeGood's first page. Delhi NCR also has a median of ₹25k. Chandigarh listings on WeddingBazaar start at ₹6.5k–12k [FACT, listings]. Salaried MUAs earn **₹1.2–6.2 LPA** nationally (AmbitionBox), and Chandigarh entry-level beauticians earn **about ₹12k/month** [CLAIM]. Urban Company partners net **₹33,469/month at >30 services** (auditor-attested) [CLAIM]. **No public data exists on time to first paid booking.** PNA must generate it from its own alumni.
3. **A family makes the decision.** In one survey, 35% of Indian students named parents as their main source of advice [FACT]. Among women outside the labour force, 44.4% cite childcare or home-making (PLFS 2025) [FACT]. The objections to design for are therefore **parental and spousal approval, safety and travel, flexible schedules, EMI, and trust in the certificate**.
4. **WhatsApp is the conversion rail.** 86% of online Indians message a business weekly, and more than 70% prefer messaging to calling, email or a website (Kantar for Meta, 2022) [FACT]. More than 60% of WhatsApp users in India message a business weekly (Meta, Q3-2023) [FACT]. Service replies are free, and click-to-WhatsApp ads open a **72-hour free messaging window** [FACT]. This directionally supports **H6**.
5. **Speed-to-lead is the cheapest lever available.** Responding in 5 minutes rather than 30 raised the odds of qualifying a lead **21×** (MIT/InsideSales, 2007) [FACT]. Responding within one hour made qualification **about 7×** more likely than waiting a further hour (HBR, 2011) [FACT]. Both are US data, but the direction is robust. This supports **H7**.
6. **Cost benchmarks are general, not makeup-specific, and come from low-reliability vendors.** India education Meta CPL runs **₹150–800**, Google Search CPL **₹250–800**, and lead-to-enrolment **3–12%** [CLAIM]. For US cross-checks, LocaliQ/WordStream report an education CPL of **$26 on Meta lead ads** and **$77 on Search** [FACT]. Webinar registrant-to-attendee conversion is **55%** (ON24, B2B) [FACT].
7. **Recommended funnel by segment** [REC]:
   - **S1 aspiring pros:** Instagram/Meta click-to-WhatsApp plus Google Search, then a counselling chat within 5 minutes, then a campus visit with a parent, then EMI.
   - **S3 working artists:** founder content, then a WhatsApp community, then a launch-style masterclass (no counselling).
   - **S2 career-switchers:** a paid ₹499–1,999 trial workshop, then counselling.
   - **S7 out-of-town:** Search, an online parents' info session and regional pop-up workshops.
   - **S5 self-makeup:** seasonal Reels and Search leading to direct booking.
8. **A low-ticket workshop leading to an upsell has an Indian beauty precedent.** Airblack sold short courses **from ₹150** and long courses **from ₹10k**. It claims **more than 35% repeat purchase** and **80% completion** [CLAIM].
9. **Trust must be proven, not claimed.** 88% of Google users read reviews, but **only 3% trust them highly** (LocalCircles) [FACT]. Proof that wins:
   - unretouched student portfolios
   - published alumni outcomes
   - practice hours, benchmarked against the NSQF standard of **510–660 hours** for national MUA qualifications [FACT]
   - batch size
   - the kit
   - **hours of founder contact**
10. **The biggest gaps are PNA's own data:** enquiry log, source mix, response times, conversion by course, and alumni earnings. Close them in **3–6 weeks** with a founder data pull, an enquiry-log audit, 15–20 student interviews, mystery shopping of 6–8 competitors and controlled paid tests (§10).

### 3.2 Segment detail and evidence notes

**S1 – Aspiring professional MUAs (18–25).**
- **Context:** urban youth (15–29) unemployment was **13.6%** in 2025 [FACT, PLFS, S-CUS-09]. A skills-based creative career with visible Instagram role models is a credible alternative [HYP].
- **Revised national qualifications:** entry into the NSQF L4 "Professional MUA" qualification now presumes prior experience or an L3 qualification. The L4 qualification needs **510 hours** (150 theory, 300 practical, 60 OJT). L5 Bridal/Fashion/Portfolio needs **660 hours** (190, 380, 90) [FACT, S-CUS-10/11]. These hours are a useful public yardstick for a "practice hours" trust claim.
- **Credibility needs:**
  - real student work (not trainer work)
  - named alumni with Instagram handles and booking proof
  - practice on live models
  - batch size
  - founder teaching hours
  - kit brand
  - certificate recognition
- **Who pays:** parents usually pay and veto [HYP, consistent with S-CUS-45].

**S2 – Career-switchers / homemakers (25–40).**
- **Labour context (PLFS 2025) [FACT, S-CUS-09]:**
  - urban female LFPR **27.7%**
  - **44.4%** of women outside the labour force cite childcare or home-making as the main reason
  - average monthly earnings are **₹18,353** for salaried women vs **₹6,374** for self-employed women

  A modest freelance makeup practice can beat typical female self-employment earnings (see §4) [EST].
- **Precedent:** Airblack reports that "a majority" of its mostly female learners became freelancers, online creators or home-salon owners [CLAIM, S-CUS-33].
- **Design requirements:** school-hours or weekend batches, modular payment, business-setup coaching (pricing, Instagram, kit sourcing), and relatable alumni of the same life stage [HYP].

**S3 – Existing artists / salon staff upskilling.**
- **Pool proxies:** about 600+ "highly rated" Chandigarh MUAs on one directory, and Delhi NCR "3000+" [FACT – listing claims, S-CUS-01/02]. About 70k nationally certified on makeup QFs in 2024-25 [FACT, S-CUS-10/11].
- **Price uplift is visible in listings:** airbrush commands +40% to +180% over traditional bridal at the same Chandigarh artist [FACT, S-CUS-03: ₹35k vs ₹25k; ₹21k vs ₹7.5k].
- **This is the segment most likely to buy "the founder"** (her signature look) rather than "an academy". That makes it the purest test of H3 [HYP].
- **Pipeline value:** S3 alumni can also become an assistant or team pipeline for the founder's own bookings in season [HYP].

**S4 – Vocational / scheme-linked.**
- **Scale and structure:** large volumes run through PMKVY, DDU-GKY and SANKALP (≈70k trained on two makeup QFs in 2024-25). The Beauty & Wellness SSC has **2.55M enrolled and 2.07M certified** cumulatively [FACT, S-CUS-12].
- **Outcomes:** placement for makeup QFs is reported as **"NA"** [FACT]. Payment is per scheme norms (Common Cost Norm Category II) [FACT].
- **Fit with PNA:** this is a B2G/CSR operating model, with mobilisation, attendance compliance, third-party assessment and placement tracking. It is not a consumer funnel, and it carries brand-dilution risk for a premium founder-led academy [HYP]. Revisit only as a CSR or partnership revenue line after core economics are proven.

**S5 – Self-makeup learners.**
- **Seasonality:** demand is highly seasonal around the wedding and festive calendar. CAIT's 2025 window was 1 Nov–14 Dec [FACT, S-CUS-13].
- **Economics for the buyer:** a family needing 3–6 guest looks across multiple functions at ₹1.5k–5k each [FACT, listings] can rationalise a ₹3k–7k workshop [EST].
- **Product fit:** these buyers do not need counselling. They need instant booking, clear dates and before/after proof on similar skin tones [HYP].

**S6 – Creators.**
- **Size:** India's influencer base was 4.06M in 2024 (Qoruz) [CLAIM, S-CUS-50]. BCG counts 2–2.5M monetised creators [CLAIM, S-CUS-51]. We found no beauty-specific count [GAP].
- **Role:** best treated as collaborators. Offer free or discounted seats in exchange for content (reach), not as a revenue segment [REC].

**S7 – Out-of-town.**
- **Why it matters:** this segment is the main lever for escaping the Tricity demand ceiling (H1/H2, to be sized by the regional agent).
- **What blocks it:** trust at a distance and parents' safety and accommodation concerns [HYP].
- **What is needed** [REC]:
  - verified accommodation partners
  - online pre-work (theory and kit familiarisation) to shorten time in Chandigarh (H4)
  - an online "parents' orientation"
  - regional alumni proof
  - intensive cohorts timed to college vacations

**S8 – Pre-migration learners.** This is a pure hypothesis, prompted by Punjab's outward-migration context. We gathered no evidence. Add "Do you plan to move abroad in the next 2 years?" to the enquiry form and interviews.

**Reading the evidence.**
1. Listed bridal prices are **starting prices of established, highly reviewed artists**. They are a ceiling reference for a new graduate, not an expectation.
2. Listings say nothing about **booking volume**. Annual income depends on bookings, which no public source measures.
3. Salaried routes give a floor of roughly **₹12–25k/month** in Chandigarh, consistent with national salary ranges.
4. **Time to first paid booking has no public evidence** [GAP]. It is the single most important ROI number for PNA to measure among its alumni.

### 5.2 Key insights with evidence

1. **Messaging-first behaviour.**
   - 86% of online Indians message a business weekly (global 66%). More than 70% prefer messaging to emailing, calling or visiting a website. 72% message businesses more since the pandemic [FACT – Kantar for Meta, 2022, S-CUS-14].
   - More than 60% of WhatsApp users in India message a business each week, and click-to-message ad revenue in India doubled year-on-year [FACT – Meta Q3-2023 earnings call, S-CUS-15].
   - **Implication:** WhatsApp is where the counselling and the conversion happen. Website forms are a secondary capture path [EST]. This supports H6 directionally.
2. **WhatsApp economics** [FACT, S-CUS-16]:
   - Service (customer-initiated) replies have been free since 1 Nov 2024. Pricing is per-message since 1 Jul 2025.
   - When a user arrives from a click-to-WhatsApp ad and the business replies within 24 hours, a **72-hour free entry-point window** opens for any message type.
   - India's marketing-template rate rose on 1 Jan 2026.
   - **Implication:** CTWA-led counselling is cheap per conversation. The real cost is **counsellor time**.
3. **Unstructured chats overwhelm counsellors.** Learnwise, a Kerala education provider, added WhatsApp Flows qualifying questions to its CTWA ads. It reports **4× qualified leads, 60% less qualifying time and 31% lower cost per qualified lead** (Oct–Nov 2025) [CLAIM – Meta success story, S-CUS-17]. **[REC]** Use 3–4 qualifying questions: course, start month, city, and whether a parent is involved.
4. **Speed-to-lead** [FACT – US data, mostly phone follow-up; directional for India and WhatsApp]:
   - MIT/InsideSales (15,000+ leads, 100,000+ call attempts, 6 firms, 2007): calling within **5 min vs 30 min** gave **100×** the odds of contact and **21×** the odds of qualification. Odds fell **>10× for contact and >6× for qualification within the first hour** [S-CUS-20]. Caveat: vendor data analysed by an academic; not peer-reviewed [S-CUS-53].
   - HBR 2011 audit of 2,241 US firms: only **37% responded within an hour**, 23% never responded and the average response was 42 hours. In a separate 1.25M-lead dataset, firms contacting within 1 hour were **~7×** more likely to qualify than those waiting a further hour, and **>60×** more likely than those waiting 24h+ [S-CUS-21/22].
   - Indian edtech: a vendor claims only the **top 15–25% of leads are called by a human within 24 hours** [CLAIM – low reliability, S-CUS-31].
   - **Implication:** response time and follow-up cadence are likely PNA's largest near-term lever. This supports H7, but magnitude must be measured in PNA's enquiry log.
5. **Show-up rates.**
   - B2B webinars: **55%** of registrants attend (35% live, 24% on-demand; ON24 2022) [FACT, S-CUS-23]. ON24 2024 data: 57% [FACT – snippet, S-CUS-24].
   - Indian edtech demos: **51–63%** show-up without reminders and **68–79%** with a voice reminder [CLAIM – low reliability, S-CUS-31].
   - Free consumer events are likely lower than B2B, and paid workshops higher [HYP, A-CUS-17/18].
6. **Paid low-ticket entry works in Indian beauty education.** Airblack ran live short courses from **₹150**, long courses from **₹10k**, and averaged a **₹24k** course fee. It claims **>35% of members buy another course**, **80% completion** (vs 5–7% for recorded courses) and NPS 72 (2021) [CLAIM, S-CUS-33]. Its 2024 one-day bridal masterclass (₹4,999) bundled 1:1 career counselling and a **50% scholarship on premium courses**. That is an explicit workshop-to-upsell design [FACT – offer page, S-CUS-34].
7. **The family is the buyer** [FACT, S-CUS-45/09]:
   - 35% of Indian students named parents as the main source of advice on course choice.
   - 44.4% of women outside the labour force cite childcare or home-making.

   **Implication:** counselling must speak to two audiences: the aspiration (student) and the risk (parent or spouse) [HYP].
8. **Reviews are read but not trusted.** LocalCircles (56k responses, 357 districts):
   - 88% of those using Google to find a business read reviews and ratings
   - only 3% have high trust in them
   - 45% find them inaccurate, and 37% find them positively biased [FACT – snippet, S-CUS-43]

   **Implication:** reviews are a hygiene factor. **Verifiable proof (student work, alumni Instagram handles, video) carries the weight** [EST].
9. **Instagram's ad audience in India skews male** (adult ad audience 29.2% female) [FACT, S-CUS-18]. The addressable female audience is still about 121M [EST]. Tight female, age and geo targeting will narrow reach and may raise CPMs [HYP]. Organic Reels and creator collaborations matter disproportionately.
10. **EMI is expected at higher tickets.** Pearl Academy offers a 6-month no-cost EMI on certificate courses including makeup, via GrayQuest, but requires full payment at seat confirmation unless financed [FACT, S-CUS-36/37]. Orane advertises no-cost EMI loans [CLAIM – snippet].

### 8.3 Worked example: why follow-up discipline beats spend (H7)
Target: **15 pro-course enrolments/month**, CPL ₹300 (A-CUS-09), fee ₹90k (A-CUS-06). All [EST].

| Scenario | Lead→enrol | Leads needed | Ad spend | CAC | CAC % of fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A – slow/irregular follow-up | 4% | 15 / 0.04 = 375 | 375 × ₹300 = ₹1,12,500 | ₹7,500 | 8.3% |
| B – <5-min response + 5-touch cadence over 14 days | 7% | 15 / 0.07 ≈ 214 | ≈ ₹64,300 | ≈ ₹4,290 (**−43%**) | 4.8% |
| C – B + referral programme (30% of enrolments, ₹3k reward) | 7% on paid | 10.5 / 0.07 = 150 | ₹45,000 + ₹13,500 rewards = ₹58,500 | ₹3,900 | 4.3% |

- Moving from A to B saves more than doubling the CPL efficiency would, and it costs only process discipline.
- Even scenario A's ~375 leads/month is ~12–13/day: one trained counsellor's load (A-CUS-25). The binding constraint is likely **who counsels (founder vs team) and how fast**, not lead volume (H3/H7).

**Positioning implications** [REC]:
1. **Lead with outcomes and proof, not certificates.** Certificates are commoditised: about 70k nationally trained on makeup QFs in a single year.

3. **Run two counselling scripts:** "career" (student) and "risk" (parent or spouse: safety, ROI distribution, refund, EMI).
4. **Treat seasonality as a sales calendar.** Target S1/S3 pre-season in Aug–Oct (enrol now, assist this season), S5 4–10 weeks before wedding and festive dates, and S1 post-results in May–Jul.
5. **Never publish best-case earnings as typical.** Publish medians with n and dates.

## 12. Gaps and caveats

1. **No PNA first-party data:** fees, catalogue, enquiry volumes, source mix, response times, conversion, completion, alumni outcomes. This is the largest gap; the V1–V2 plan closes it.
2. **Time to first paid booking and year-1 earnings for new MUAs:** no public source found. Listed prices reflect established artists only.
3. **India education CPL and conversion benchmarks** come only from agency/vendor blogs (low reliability). **No makeup- or beauty-education-specific Indian funnel benchmarks** were found.
4. **WhatsApp Business India user count:** no official figure. We used Meta's "60% of WhatsApp users in India message a business weekly" (Q3-2023) and the Kantar 2022 survey instead.
5. **Chandigarh accommodation costs and safety perceptions** for out-of-town students were not verified (S7).
6. **Govt-scheme economics (S4):** payout per candidate, placement obligations and partner accreditation were not researched in depth. Placement for makeup QFs is reported "NA".
7. **Beauty-specific creator counts (S6)** and **pre-migration learner share (S8)** are unknown.
8. **Several sources were captured from search snippets** after the search quota ran out. The register marks them "snippet"; re-verify before slide use. These are mainly the WeddingBazaar profiles, AmbitionBox, WorkIndia, Sotros, LocalCircles, Nav Brar and Shiksha.
9. **US benchmarks** (LocaliQ, MIT/HBR, ON24) are used only directionally. India-specific consumer webinar and demo show-up data is weak.
10. **Regulatory:** applicability of Indian consumer-protection guidance on misleading education/coaching advertising (e.g., placement or income claims) was not verified. Refer to counsel before publishing outcome claims.
